# 7. Routes and the final choice

*Two ways down out of the mountains. One is fast and takes the wagon away from you. One keeps the wagon and takes the time. Neither is the safe one.*

This is where the campaign collects its debts. Every promise still open, every favor earned and unspent, every bundle somebody insisted on keeping since Independence — it all comes to the same table at The Dalles, and the party divides what arrives from what does not. Show both routes in full before anyone pays for either. The last stretch of this journey should be decided by people who understood the offer, not by people who guessed.

Version 0.10. Use the fictionalized [map](docs/TRAIL_MAP.md). Costs are game values. The GM states them before commitment. Route promises may be incomplete, but the Guide can investigate, and a false claim must have discoverable evidence.

## Sublette decision, marker 790

Seventy miles against a fort you promised somebody you would reach. The road forks; the obligation does not.

Present before Fort Bridger (850): established route with trade and a promised delivery, or a shortcut skipping that fort. This is the chapter 4 major decision, replacing that day's random event.

- **Established:** continue through Bridger and marker 900 normally. Keep access to the fort and its services.
- **Shortcut:** saves 70 miles; after completing a 40-mile waterless stretch, advance the route marker by an additional 70. Do not add calendar days for the skipped miles. Rejoin at marker 900, then begin chapter 5. Fort Bridger remains unvisited. Cross the 40 miles at actual modified speed; no fixed three-day promise if oxen or weather slow you.
- **Forecast:** pay ordinary water each travel/wait day, 4 gallons in heat. The shortcut has no refill until rejoining. GM shows the current mileage/water estimate including speed penalties before departure. No additional automatic CON check or stock damage; ordinary weather and one event for the crossing apply.

If a promise needs Bridger, the party can delay the shortcut, ask another named traveler to deliver, or openly break it. Delegation requires their agreement; an NPC is not a guaranteed remote action. Record the consequence.

## The false cutoff rumor

Somebody on this road is always sure they know a better way. This one is wrong, and the game does not punish the party for listening — only for refusing to check.

A traveler claims a second shortcut saves weeks. This is fictional rumor content, not a runnable Hastings route in this Oregon campaign. Show contradictory landmarks and an eastbound report immediately. Guide Read the ground confirms the route does not serve the destination. No secret fourteen-day punishment for following a GM-provided hook. If players knowingly want a separate disaster expedition, end this campaign's route choice there and agree its new scope before play.

## Finale at The Dalles, marker 1,900

One hundred miles left, and the wagon that carried everything this far is now itself a thing to be spent or kept.

Reveal both routes before anyone pays. These fixed-duration journeys replace the last 100 miles. Use weather/food/conditions on their days but **no random trail events**: the three prepared obstacles below are the finale's events. Weather can extend the road's duration if it prevents travel; weather speed multipliers do not otherwise multiply these already abstract fixed durations. River passage waits out a blizzard before launching. At arrival set marker 2,000.

Before choosing, resolve one actual relationship return from [People](docs/PEOPLE.md). This changes whom the party intends to meet or help after arrival, not the published route fees. Then review the People Ledger. A specifically earned favor provides its listed help, once. If a helpful person cannot plausibly be present, their named contact or message can be, established before payment. Do not resurrect dead allies or conjure unknown favors.

### Columbia passage — 7 travel days

*Fast water and a week. You go down the river with what fits in the boat, and the wagon stays behind either as cash or as lumber.*

At The Dalles, use Boise’s stock, posted prices, resale rules, and labor rates; this is a final service stop, not another chapter endpoint reward. The party sells/caches wagon and teams, then travels by carrier or builds a raft. Purchased carrier passage **$80 party total**, includes safe handling and cargo up to four bundles plus personal items/bedding. Additional bundles $10 each. For both carrier and raft, food occupies one bundle per 50 lbs (round up), ammunition one per 40 rounds, parts one each, and up to 10 medicine one; money fits a free pouch. Personal clothes/keepsakes and Warm Bedding are free passage cargo. Carrier food is not included; count party rations/water normally, water refill available daily. Buying passage needs no roll. Local resale follows Boise's multiplier and half-value rule; wagon resale is $20 flat if usable, $0 if unusable. A full-health or injured living team sells at its posted resale value; no price exploit from buying teams locally.

Without sufficient cash, build a raft from the dismantled wagon (which cannot be sold or recovered), or spend one additional labor day gathering timber if no wagon is available. Building then takes one preparation day (additional, no travel), then seven river days. Teamster spends 1 part for a sound raft, guaranteed, or STR DC 12; failure requires one more preparation day and then succeeds. Raft has four cargo-bundle spaces plus personal items and bedding; food rounds up into 50-lb bundles, ammo into 40-round bundles, parts one each, medicine up to 10 doses one bundle, money one free pouch. No water barrels required on this route; daily safe refill is available.

On river days 2, 4, and 6, describe a narrowing channel. Choose **portage**, adding one labor day with no check, or **run it**, a group DEX/WIS DC 12 check. Full success pass; partial pass and lose one chosen cargo bundle (if none remains, each participant loses 1 HP instead); failure reach the bank, each participant takes 1d4 damage, and add one recovery/portage day. No unannounced drowning. Portage days can receive ordinary treatment but no Rest recovery. Earned river intelligence lowers one run's DC to 10; an earned experienced pilot grants Advantage on one run. Carrier passage resolves these professionally without checks or portage delays. On those days invite a different player to describe what they are preparing for arrival, including any recorded agreement. No surprise hazard is added to punish a safely purchased journey.

### Barlow Road — 14 travel days

*Two weeks over the mountain, a toll at the gate, and a descent at Laurel Hill that has taken better wagons than yours. You keep everything you brought — if it keeps up.*

**Game toll: $25 for wagon and stock together**, or two parts, or two cargo bundles accepted at the gate. These are invented balancing prices, not historical tolls. If none is available, perform two labor days at the gate (food paid normally), then passage is permitted. The route never becomes an unplayable paywall. Wagon and teams remain yours. Travelers already on foot can take this road without a wagon toll, using the same scheduled days and personal carrying limits; bypass wagon-specific choices/checks at days 3 and 8 by spending one extra walking day at each. All other costs still apply.

On day 3, a blocked grade: spend one extra labor day clearing it, or unload one bundle permanently to use a bypass. On day 8, Laurel Hill: spend one part and one extra labor day fixing anchors for guaranteed descent, or group STR/DEX DC 12. Full success pass; partial pass, wagon loses 3 HP; failure wagon loses 1d6+3 HP and add one Repair Day. If unusable, repair by ordinary rules on that day or later; extra failed-repair days extend arrival. If it cannot be repaired, cache wagon cargo and finish the **remaining scheduled road days** on foot with ordinary carrying limits, plus two additional days. Do not charge those two days repeatedly. On day 12, a shelter is crowded: give 10 lbs food toward shared supplies for safe rest that night, or use normal outdoor shelter/weather rules. Only one night, not a new recurring fee.

An earned repair favor can supply the Laurel Hill part or restore its stated HP afterward, once. A prior shelter-building favor grants free shared shelter on day 12. Players' protected personal cargo must be assigned to the bundles before deciding what to leave.

### Arrival

The last mile is not a test. It is an accounting.

No final surprise roll decides whether the expedition counts. Finish the chosen route, resolve [Arrival and promises](docs/ARRIVAL.md), then give each person their scene. The route's mechanical outcome supplies constraints; the player determines what their character hopes and chooses next.
