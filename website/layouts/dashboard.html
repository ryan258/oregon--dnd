💡 Before You Start (10 minutes)
  1. Print the handouts. Press 🖨️ Print Handouts at the top. You get 5 large-print character cards and 1 River Crossing Menu.
  2. Hand out the cards. Give each player one card. Put the River Menu in the middle of the table. Keep a d20 (20-sided die) where everyone can reach it.
  3. Explain the only rule. Say: “When something is risky, roll the d20 and add the number on your card. 10 or higher works.”
  4. Agree on a break. Plan one 10-minute break after the vote in Step 2. Anyone can ask for a break at any time.
  5. Press Next ▶ when you're ready. Read the boxes with the brown edge out loud. The green 💡 boxes are just for you.

Golden rules for your first game

  • You can't do it wrong. If you forget a rule, decide what feels fair and keep going.
  • Ask, don't tell. When it goes quiet, ask one player: “What does your character do?”
  • Only roll when it's risky. Talking, helping, and buying things don't need dice.
  • Mistakes are easy to fix. ↶ Undo takes back the last button press.

❓ Quick Answers (open when something comes up)
How do I handle a roll?
The player rolls the d20 and adds their skill number (+2, +1, or +0). 10 or more succeeds. The risky Upper Ford needs 12 or more. Say what happens if they fail before they roll.
Which number does a player add?
Look at their card. +2 is what they're best at, +1 is what they're good at, and anything else is +0. If you're not sure, use +0.
What does “Inspiration” do?
Each player can use it once: they roll twice and keep the higher number.
Someone wants to do something that isn't listed.
Say yes if it makes sense. If it's risky, ask for a roll (10+). If it costs something, say what it costs before they decide.
A traveler drops to 0 HP.
They are hurt, not dead. They can't act until another traveler spends their turn helping them back up to 1 HP. The player keeps talking for their character. Tonight, nobody dies from a single bad roll.
The table is arguing too long.
Say: “Let's vote. Point at the option you like.” Majority wins. Note the reasons in Playtest Notes.
I need a break, or my hands or eyes are tired.
Take one. Say “Let's pause for 10 minutes.” The dashboard saves everything automatically. Turn on A+ Large Text if the screen is hard to read.
I pressed the wrong button.
Press ↶ Undo at the top. You can press it several times.

Read Aloud · The River Queue

“June 2, 1848. The Platte River is wide, brown, and loud. Twenty wagons are stuck in the mud line. Ferryman Ellis Bell’s sign reads: $10 A WAGON — NEXT BOAT TOMORROW. Right in front of you, three things happen:”

Clara & Eliza

Schoolteacher Mercy Reed is $4 short and about to be shoved out of line. She asks Eliza to borrow a schoolbook to calm the children. Clara can use her free power to cut our ferry price from $10 to $8.

Thomas & Cal

Neighbor Ada Pike has river fever and needs Thomas (1 Medicine, roll 10+). Meanwhile, Mercy offers Cal a book for 10 lbs of food (or he can hunt on the bluff).

Mae

Across the river, Mara Vale holds up a painted workshop sign with Mae’s name on it—her wagon train leaves at dawn on Day 3!

💡 Your job right now (about 15 minutes)

  1. Point to the River Crossing Menu in the middle of the table. Read the box below out loud.
  2. Let the players talk it over. You don't need to pick for them. Every option is fine.
  3. When they agree, press their option below. The dashboard pays the costs and moves you to Step 3.

If they can't decide: say “Let's vote: point at the option you like,” and go with the majority.
Clara's bargain (1A): only pick this if Clara's player offered to haggle with the ferryman. It's free and always works.
If they choose Option 3 (the Ford): in Step 3, everyone needs 12+ instead of 10+.

💡 Your job right now (about 20 minutes)

  1. Read the box below out loud. Before anyone rolls, tell them: “If 3 or more of you succeed, we cross cleanly. If only 1 or 2 succeed, we lose something small.”
  2. Go around the table in the order shown. Each player rolls the d20 and adds +2. Read their line in the green box for them, or let them describe what they do.
  3. For each player who reaches 10 or more (12+ at the Ford), tap their name below. The dashboard counts the successes and tells you what happens.

Partial success? Let the players choose what's lost: 1 cargo bundle (for example, 50 lbs of food or 1 spare part) or one volunteer loses 1–2 HP. Update the numbers with the − buttons.
Celebrate the rolls. A high roll deserves a cheer and a low roll gets a sympathetic groan. Keep it fun.
Move on when: the result is read out. Press Next ▶.

Mid-River Action · Everyone Rolls Once! (Need 10+ with their +2 skill)

“Halfway across the swollen river, an uprooted cottonwood tree surges straight toward the wagon! Go around the table—each player rolls the 20-sided die and adds +2:”

1. Eliza (+2)

Spot the hidden gravel bar before the current swings the wheels!

2. Mae (+2)

Hold the lead oxen steady as cold water hits their chests!

3. Cal (+2)

Catch the drifting rope and book crate before the river takes it!

4. Thomas (+2)

Keep feverish Ada and Mercy steady when the wagon lurches!

5. Clara (+2)

Call out “Heave on three!” so everyone pulls together!

💡 Your job right now (about 15 minutes, then wrap up)

  1. Read the campfire box out loud. Nobody needs to roll. This step is about the characters.
  2. Ask the closing question. Go around the circle, and let anyone pass.
  3. If the table wants a treat, press Serve Clara's Proper Meal.

Wrapping up: thank everyone. Ask: “What was your favorite moment?” and “Would you play again?” Type the answers in Quick Playtest Notes (Step 4 below), then press Download Notes.
If time is short: skip straight to the closing question. That's the part people remember.

3. Five Travelers

Each player starts at 9 HP and adds +2 to their specialty, +1 to good skills, and +0 to most others.

💡 How to read a card: the skill line tells you what number a player adds to a roll. HP is health: press − when someone gets hurt. Tired goes up only if the story says so, such as a skipped meal or a hard push. Tick Chapter power used once a player uses their special once-per-chapter power. Open Story & powers to remind a player what their character wants.
