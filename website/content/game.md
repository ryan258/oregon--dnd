Everything you own is in one wagon. The destination is shared. Your reasons for going are not.

Oregon Trail RPG is a standalone survival tabletop game for **one Trailmaster (the GM) and 1–4 players controlling four travelers**, or solo. It uses familiar d20 checks. You do not need D&D books or experience with this setting.

## What you actually do

The Trailmaster describes the road, the people on it, and the costs the party can see. Players decide where to go, whom to help, and what to risk. Roll when an outcome is threatened; routine professional work with suitable time and tools succeeds.

A day might mean traveling, hunting, repairing, trading, or resting. Each choice uses time. Supplies, weather, health, and promises make that time matter. At camp, people mend things, disagree, tell stories, and decide why they are still going together.

## Four roles. Four reasons to keep going.

The **Guide** reads the ground. The **Doctor** cares for people. The **Hunter** finds food and stretches stores. The **Teamster** keeps the wagon moving. Everyone also has a Dream, a Promise, a Boundary, a Bond, and a small keepsake.

Those personal stakes belong in the decisions. Saving a crate of books can matter as much as saving a barrel of flour. A partnership can be worth a day's delay. Nobody needs to agree on the price.

## Start at the river

In **The River Between**, a ferryman posts his price while a schoolteacher counts money she does not have. You can pay for passage tomorrow, risk the ford today, or work two days to earn a crossing. A carrier leaves on a schedule, with someone one traveler hoped to meet.

Use the four ready-to-play travelers and the standalone river supplies. The adventure targets a single 2–3 hour session; that duration still needs table testing.

## Or take the whole road

Seven chapters follow an abstracted 2,000-mile journey from Independence to Oregon: plains, rivers, mountains, a desert crossing, the Snake River, and the final choice at The Dalles. The full campaign targets 6–8 sessions. Pace and balance remain playtest questions.

You learn techniques at the end of chapters 2 and 5. You do not gain bigger pools of hit points. Growth lives in expertise, relationships, and what people choose to carry forward.

## The table's promise

- Risks and catastrophic stakes are disclosed before commitment.
- Players control their travelers' decisions. Bookkeeping can be delegated.
- Death is permanent, but the player immediately takes a reserve or replacement traveler.
- Agree on tone, boundaries, and ways to pause or change a scene before starting.
- Arrival asks what life these people want now, using the promises they actually made.

This is historical fiction set on inhabited Indigenous lands. Distances, economics, medical rules, and scenario people are game abstractions. The handbook includes historical framing and sources.
