# People who remember

*Some people travel beside you. Some stay where their lives already are. Both remember.*

Mercy, Tomas, and Nessa are not quest-givers and not rescue services. They are travelers with the same problem the party has — too much to carry, not enough time — who happen to be going the same way. They remember what was done for them and what was not, they tell other people, and the favors they offer are specific, single-use, and limited to what a person in their circumstances could actually provide. Help them because it costs you something. That is the only reason the game rewards.

Version 0.10. These fictional people supply their own parties and households. The Hall stock keeper has a regional life and does not follow the PCs to Oregon. They are not extra player actions, stockpiles, or guaranteed rescuers. Introduce their needs before asking for help; tell players when a favor is earned. Record specific promises, favors, and knowledge on the [ledger](sheets/people_ledger.md).

## Mercy Reed — a schoolteacher with a wagon of books

**Wants:** to keep a traveling lending library and lessons available to adults who cannot settle yet. She values putting books to work now, which can conflict with Eliza's wish to deliver her collection intact. **Can offer:** letters, contacts, organizational work. **Will not:** abandon a person to save the books. **Voice:** asks precise questions, then waits for an answer. Her adult brother Jonah drives their wagon; no child peril is needed.

- Chapter 1: asks the party to carry one crate (one named cargo bundle) until Kearny while a wheel is repaired. Delivering it earns **Mercy's introduction**: a named contact at Laramie will meet that fort's demand on the party's behalf. Spend once; still roll the bargain.
- Chapter 2: short $10 for passage. Pay her $10 shortfall or complete the river adventure's two-day labor agreement. Earn **river intelligence**: her collected crossing notes lower one finale raft-run DC to 10, once. She can also provide the report needed at Laramie; this is the same information, not extra supplies.
- Chapter 5: if helped, she leaves a verified water report at the previous stop. It identifies the 90-mile source in chapter 5 as safe, removing the need to investigate that source. It does not refill the party's barrels.
- Finale: if alive and on good terms, asks which book the party thinks the traveling library should lend next. The answer becomes an epilogue connection, not a new automatic reward.

**If refused:** she sells books to pay passage, arrives with fewer, and remembers honestly without becoming a villain. **If a promise is broken:** she asks for restitution or an explanation; no omniscient knowledge.

## Tomas Fitch — a wheelwright protecting a heavy tool chest

**Wants:** a shared repair service where each worker has a say; at Hall he will decide whether to stay and start it. Mae may prefer sole ownership and a workshop farther west. **Can offer:** repairs, work, practical instruction. **Will not:** lend his last tools without a return agreement. **Voice:** checks the object while listening to its owner.

- Chapter 1: willing to exchange one ordinary repair (restore 4 Wagon HP, once) for 20 lbs food; no roll.
- Chapter 3: needs help saving his tool load in the campaign's slipping-wagon scene. A successful rescue earns **Tomas's repair favor**: at a later meeting he supplies one part for a specific repair or restores 4 Wagon HP. The favor is single-use, not both.
- Chapter 4: his tool chest makes the shortcut difficult. Carry it as a named bundle to Hall or tell him honestly you cannot. Delivery earns no second mechanical repair favor if one is already held; it fulfills a significant promise for Morale and establishes trust.
- Finale: he can have left the promised repair part with a named road contact. Establish this at The Dalles before the players choose a route.

**If refused:** he hires other help and arrives later. **If tools are lost:** he will accept labor or a replacement plan; the party cannot buy forgiveness with one CHA roll.

## Nessa Bell — a carrier trying to keep a delivery promise

**Wants:** an independent carrier route reliable enough that customers plan around it. She keeps stated departures even when friends want more time. **Can offer:** reliable route reports and introductions. **Will not:** carry sealed dangerous or stolen goods without knowing what she accepts. **Voice:** writes agreements in a small book and reads them back.

- Chapter 2: can pass a message to the next fort for $1; leaves the far bank at dawn on scene day 3 with Mara. Messages move with her, not instantaneously. Her announced schedule creates the river meeting window; see the adventure for weather delays.
- Chapter 4: her Hall delivery gives Ruth at Bridger a reason to offer a bargain. Accepting creates a specific obligation, delivery to Nessa by Hall departure. Arriving late does not make her disappear; a named clerk can receive it.
- Chapter 5: a freight partner has not arrived. Bring a truthful report from the road or help search one day; this meets Hall's demand.
- Chapter 6: asks for a day repairing a shared waystation shelter. Helping earns **shelter credit**: free shared shelter on Barlow day 12. If choosing the river, her carrier contact instead discounts passage by $10. Choose one benefit, once.

**If refused:** hires help when she can. **If lied to:** correct the record before seeking another favor. NPC travel follows the fiction; do not place her simultaneously at two forts.

Resolve these at the named scene, after telling the party what is at stake. They do not erase earned favors or force PC agreement.

No book gift is claimed if none occurred. |
| Tomas | At Hall, decide whether to begin a shared repair service there. | If the Hall work-area terms are accepted and tools are available, stays and sends any earned repair favor west through a named carrier. | Continues west seeking paid repair work; no punishment for refusing partnership. | At The Dalles, either his letter reports the service's first job, or he meets the party as an independent worker. |

A chosen long-term partnership is an intention with named terms, not guaranteed money, land, or success. News needs a plausible carrier and time. If the party outruns news, receive it later or at Arrival instead of teleporting the NPC.

## The Hall stock keeper — work that will continue after the wagons leave

**Portrayal:** a fictional Shoshone adult woman, known to these travelers by her requested working address, “stock keeper.” This English label is not a translated Indigenous name. Her exact household, scenes, speech, work arrangement, and meetings are invented. Regional grounding and its limits are in [History](HISTORY.md); no specialist review is claimed. Do not add a ceremony, accent, language, or claim to speak for a nation.

**Wants:** return her household's animals and equipment in good condition and keep a usable work area near Hall. **Can offer:** her own time, a specific shared workspace by agreement, and a message about work done there. **Will not:** displace her household's work to give paid freight automatic priority. **Voice:** asks who will do a job and when the borrowed object comes back. **Pleasure:** insists that a good sign should be legible to someone passing it, and enjoys correcting Tomas's elaborate designs.

- At the chapter 5 spring, she and the damaged-wagon party need to use the same approach. Use the campaign's repair-or-report decision; clean water stays available. She leaves for Hall when her own work is ready. If the PCs arrive before her, the work-area discussion waits until her arrival or occurs by her named household representative, with the same stated terms.
- At Hall, she offers shared use of the work area. She negotiates with Tomas, Mae, and Nessa as individuals; a payment never gives access to unrelated ground or a general community favor.
- After the party leaves, she continues her household's work whether or not Tomas stays. A later carried letter reports the actual arrangement and asks what became of the party's sign. She does not abandon her life to deliver it personally.

**If refused:** she uses the area with her household and arranges repairs another way. **If an agreement is broken:** she sends a specific account and asks for the promised return or correction. Neither a reaction roll nor failed bargaining turns this into automatic violence. This relationship has no extra stat bonus; its consequence is what people were able to do together and what remains owed.

## Mara Vale — a partnership with terms

Mara is Mae's fictional former coworker, introduced with that player's agreement. She travels with Nessa and wants a workshop partner willing to divide responsibilities and put both names on its sign. She offers a discussion, never a free technique or starter supplies. At the river meeting, ask Mae to name one term; Mara accepts equal responsibility, a limited trial period, or separate benches with shared overhead. She declines ownership terms giving her no say. No roll is needed to hear or accept those limits.

If Mae misses the meeting, Mara looks for another partner on the journey. A delivered letter earns a reply and a later conversation, not a reserved business share. At The Dalles a named carrier can relay her current address; at Arrival she offers the discussion that was actually agreed. If the player rejected this connection, leave Mara as another traveler rather than manufacturing an obligation.

## Meeting someone again

Nobody stands still waiting to be met. Between appearances they have travelled, traded, lost things, and heard news — arrive with what changed.

On a recurring-person event choose one whose return is plausible. Ask: What changed for them? What did they hear, and from whom? What do they need that the party cannot solve for free? If no meeting is plausible, find a letter, a marked campsite, or a mutual acquaintance only when their travel permits it; otherwise use quiet progress and let the news arrive later. Favor redemption needs an actual opportunity; a ledger mark never works as magic.

## Reserve travelers

Jonah Reed, mechanic Ruth Ames traveling with Tomas, and carrier Ellis Bell traveling with Nessa can serve as temporary/replacement PCs with player permission. Their names, occupations, and connections are fictional. Use the chosen role's fixed-array sheet; no supplies materialize. A fourth reserve can be named by the players. NPC stat blocks are unnecessary until someone becomes an active traveler.
