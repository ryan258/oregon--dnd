# The River Between

*Ten dollars, chalked on a board. A ferryman who charges everyone the same. A schoolteacher counting coins on a crate of books, coming up short. And a ford upstream that will take you across today, for free, for a price.*

Put the four crossing offers in front of the party in the first ten minutes. A part buys a safe ford, labor buys passage for Mercy too, and a day's delay may cost a meeting. Let the travelers decide what they are willing to spend before anyone touches a die.

**A complete introductory chapter for Oregon Trail RPG v0.10.** One GM, 1–4 players controlling four travelers, target 2–3 hours. This duration is untested. Use the [pregens](PREGENS.md), [reference](sheets/gm_reference.md), [wagon sheet](sheets/party_wagon_sheet.md), and [people ledger](sheets/people_ledger.md). All people and local scene sites are fictional.

## Five-minute start

Set out the [river decision card](sheets/river_decision_card.md). It gives the four offers, the optional scouting day, and the prices, risks, and departure the party needs to hear before choosing.

Give everyone a character. Read their Dreams and Promises aloud, ask agreement on Bonds, and allow changes. Confirm boundaries using [session zero](SESSION_ZERO.md). Tell the table that everyone keeps playing and that they will hear the stakes before choosing. Use the card's Trailmaster reference for Rest, mixed ramp work, and weather during the introduction.

Standalone start: **June 2, marker 340**, Morale 6; 140 lbs cured food, no fresh food, 60 gallons capacity/full (water not tracked here), 4 medicine, 40 ammunition, 2 parts, **$25**, Warm Bedding, tools, wagon 22/30, three teams at 8/10 each. Use pregens' full HP, Exhaustion 0, one Inspiration. A crate of Eliza's books and Mae's tools are named personal cargo bundles; no other cargo inventory is needed. These replace the campaign's starting purchases, not additional goods.

Campaign entry uses actual state and starts the scene when the party reaches 340. If the party arrives in a different month, use actual weather. For the standalone, first three days are Clear and Mild; roll weather beginning June 5. Subsequent weather follows the core rules.

## Three locations

The ferry and ford reach the same far-bank camp. Scouting, waiting, and working use days at marker 340.

```text
340 — QUEUE CAMP: names, needs, accurate prices, safe Rest possible
         | one local scouting day; no route-marker progress
         +—— UPPER FORD: inspect it before choosing; return to camp free
         |
         +—— FERRY LANDING: loading work and payment
                   |
                   +—— far-bank marker 358 — road onward to Laramie 500
```

A completed crossing uses one Travel Day and advances **18 miles to 358** regardless of stance. Use the price and any check quoted for the chosen approach. The [decision card](sheets/river_decision_card.md) gives the weather exceptions and the group check for an unprepared ford.

## Scene 1 — A price everyone can see

Read or paraphrase: “The crossing rope hums in the current. On a board beside the landing, somebody has written TEN DOLLARS. Mercy Reed is counting coins beside an open crate of books. The operator points to the same price for everyone.”

**Operator Ellis Ward:** wants to clear the queue safely; refuses overloaded runs; no secret hostility. **Mercy:** $10 short, owns books she can sell, refuses to leave her brother behind. **Tomas:** can help build a loading ramp but needs another able worker. **Nessa:** her freight is already across. She visits this bank by passenger boat and returns tonight; passenger travel cannot carry the party wagon or shortcut its crossing. She leaves the far-bank camp at dawn on the third scene day (June 4 standalone), taking a faster contract to Laramie. She can carry a message for $1.

Show [all four crossing offers](sheets/river_decision_card.md#crossing-offers) before the party commits. Give the optional scouting quote with the ford. The Guide's Read the ground covers the same scouting day and grants the card's Advantage. A traveler who scouts uses their day on that action. Ellis takes payment at boarding, with no social roll or advance booking. The first sailing is day 2, June 3 in the standalone. After that, announce the next available sailing before the party spends another day.

### The departure that makes tomorrow matter

Before choosing, show Nessa's departure date beside the crossing prices. Mae's former coworker **Mara Vale** travels with Nessa's freight and is offering to share a workshop in the Valley. Mae can discuss ownership and working terms face to face at the far-bank camp on either of the first two evenings. Mara wants to divide responsibility now because she will seek another partner on the road. The meeting grants no cash, technique, or guaranteed business success; Mae can accept, negotiate, or decline. Record the actual terms for arrival.

Ramp work misses that meeting on the posted schedule. If weather changes Nessa's departure, announce Mara's last available evening again before the next commitment. Mae may send a written proposal with Nessa from the queue on day 1 for $1. Nessa delivers it; Mara's default response is a written invitation to find her at the Valley and discuss whatever work is then available, with no partnership reserved. No hidden persuasion check or permanently ruined dream. Mae can propose another arrangement now; Ellis can relay a message after departure, but cannot recall the carrier. In an ongoing campaign offer this connection to the player whose Dream fits, with their agreement; if no one wants it, use Nessa's openly stated departure as an optional opportunity and record that the deadline did not matter to this table.

### Four reasons to speak

Use these within the decision, not as four compulsory side quests. Players may reject the hook or substitute their own stake.

- **Guide:** Mercy asks to use one of Eliza's mother's lesson books while the queue waits today, before any crossing. Eliza can lead a short lesson, invite someone else to lead, or keep the book packed. Pack it again before leaving unless Eliza explicitly lends it beyond the lesson or gives it away. This uses no separate day or action.
- **Doctor:** adult traveler **Ada Pike** asks for ordinary treatment of dysentery. She has 6/9 HP, 3 marks, 1 HP of damage due today, and a caretaker with their own provisions. Thomas can spend 1 medicine and roll INT DC 10 before leaving the queue, alongside today's chosen action. On success Ada still takes 1 HP after rounding up, and her caretaker rolls Ada's evening recovery with Advantage (CON +0, DC 10, one mark removed on success). On failure, use the same damage and a single recovery die. If Thomas takes a Rest Day here, he can use his Camp Action for Stabilize instead. Ada's party handles later care. Refusal creates no scripted death or penalty.
- **Hunter:** Mercy offers to trade one of her remaining books for 10 lbs cured food, with no cash value assigned to the book. Cal can give the food instead, trade, or keep the reserve. This neither pays her crossing nor earns river intelligence; it changes who keeps that book. Nearby game is available in the standalone if Cal chooses to hunt instead of labor or Rest.
- **Teamster:** Mae owns the response to Mara's offer and can explain what the prepared rope line would buy. The rest of the table decides the shared wagon's approach together; her role never grants a veto over other PCs.

No one forces a charitable choice. If refused, Mercy sells books to cross later. The consequences are her changed prospects and the absence of a favor, not a moral penalty imposed by the GM.

## Scene 2 — The crossing

Whatever they chose, make the crossing itself a scene about hands and cargo, not about dice. Ask what each person carries across personally.

For the ferry, completed ramp labor, or a prepared rope line, ask each traveler what they insist on unloading or securing themselves. Then cross safely. For a ford attempt without the rope line, describe the current and read all three group check outcomes from the [decision card](sheets/river_decision_card.md#crossing-offers). Scouting and a traveler's own Inspiration can each provide Advantage under the usual stacking rules. Apply Wound rules if damage reaches 0 HP.

**Every role has a decision:** Guide chooses information versus time; Teamster decides whether the part is worth a guaranteed crossing; Doctor reserves medicine for a patient or spends their day's effort helping; Hunter weighs waiting against a hunt. A Hunter who hunts on a waiting day cannot also repair the ramp or Rest. No game is guaranteed at the queue; nearby game is available in the standalone, so Take Aim is a valid alternative.

## Scene 3 — What crosses with you

The far bank is quieter than anyone expects. Wet rope, a fire, and the first real chance to count what came over with them.

At the first far-bank camp, first resolve the Mara meeting, or record any letter actually sent and let a reply arrive later through a plausible carrier. Then let someone attempt to teach the others a useless skill: whistling, folding a paper boat, or imitating an ox. No check, bonus, or surprise threat; a player may simply describe enjoying it. Each player answers or passes: “What did you hand to somebody else because you trusted them more than the river?” A fulfilled significant promise grants its one Morale. If helping Mercy cost time or goods, award the involved character's Inspiration if eligible.

Mercy gives her accurate river report if her crossing was paid or the passage labor completed. Record **river intelligence**: lower one finale raft-run DC to 10. This same report meets Laramie's demand; using it there does not destroy the knowledge. Tomas does not also grant a repair favor merely for being present; that requires his chapter 3 help.

If a personal bundle was lost, its owner decides what the loss means for their dream. A tool chest can be replaced by a new promise to work for tools; a lost book crate does not erase the character's reason to continue.

## Finishing the chapter

Eight ordinary days of road to Fort Laramie. This is the group's first taste of the campaign's real rhythm: batch the miles, spend the food, stop when something asks a question.

From far-bank marker 358 to Laramie 500 is 142 miles: eight ordinary 18-mile travel days, stopping at 500 rather than overshooting. Use two three-day legs and one two-day leg, with one event per leg; short final legs still receive one event. On chapter 2 event-table results that would introduce another major crossing, reinterpret the site as a minor side-channel with the **same disclosed mechanics**, or replace it openly with a traveler message before rolling. Do not force an extra major set piece just to fill time.

The far-bank camp is a complete first-session ending. Follow the [card's pause instructions](sheets/river_decision_card.md#weather-and-the-next-session) to resume with the same state. For the remaining road and Laramie trade, use the Complete handbook's campaign references. Award Laramie arrival and chapter advancement when the party reaches marker 500.

At Laramie, endpoint Morale +1, trade/rest if desired, and one technique per PC. Owen Bell accepts an accurate crossing report for his demand. Use the [worked trade](../03_settlements_and_trading.md) if needed. Finish by changing or affirming a personal statement and naming one person to see again.

## Six optional complications

Use the six chapter 2 events in [Events](../01_events_and_hazards.md) for the onward road. During the three prepared scenes, use **no random event**. If the queue needs texture, describe a dropped cup, a repair argument, or another wagon's arrival without charging a second surprise hazard.

## GM answers and recovery

Every failure state here has a printed way onward. Nobody gets stranded at 340 because the dice were unkind or the purse was empty.

- **No cash or parts?** Ramp labor still earns passage on the next running ferry. If exactly one PC can work, Tomas supplies the second worker for three labor days, with crossing on day 4 at the earliest. Quote the actual departure and letter option before accepting. If none can work, Mercy sells books to fund the next available ferry, asking for an explicit future favor. Use reserves or shared roles immediately so everyone can play.
- **Wagon stopped or too few able travelers?** The ferry crew can load the party's wagon and surviving teams even if the party cannot pull it. This is a crossing exception. Ordinary wagon travel resumes after the party restores the wagon, usable teams, and able travelers as needed. Both ford options require the party to be able to move its own wagon.
- **Two injured PCs?** Use reserve travelers immediately. Count active slots and food explicitly. Tomas can care for a patient with his party while the reserve occupies the active slot, if agreed.
- **Failed ford?** Close the failed day at 340 and offer the remaining choices next morning. If the first ferry is running June 3, the party can pay and board that day after a June 2 failure. Charge the failed ford and the crossing as two separate days.
- **Party splits over payment?** Give each player one statement of priorities, then agree the shared wagon's plan. No CHA roll overrides a player.
- **Players invent a winch, raft, or barter?** Give the relevant person a credible reason to agree or decline, then state a time/material cost and DC before commitment. Equivalent preparation should buy equivalent safety.

## Debrief

Use [Playtest](PLAYTEST.md). Record when the choice stopped being obvious, whether the departure mattered, any player-proposed approach, each role's actual decision, and minutes spent on accounting. Ask which choice felt real, whether every role mattered, and which person they want to meet again. Record actual elapsed time and rules lookups; do not rate the game for the players.
