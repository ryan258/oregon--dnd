# Glossary

A quick-reference guide to abbreviations, TTRPG jargon, and game-specific terms used in the Oregon Trail RPG. Organized for table use — if you're new to tabletop RPGs or this system, start here.

---

## Dice Notation

| Notation | Meaning |
| -------- | ------- |
| **d20** | A twenty-sided die. The core resolution die — roll it, add your modifier, compare to the DC. |
| **d10** | A ten-sided die. Used for segment-specific event tables. |
| **d6** | A six-sided die. Used for disease rolls, weather, stat generation, and various effects. |
| **d4** | A four-sided die. Used for healing, damage, and minor effects. |
| **3d6** | Roll three six-sided dice and sum them. The number before the "d" is how many dice to roll. |

---

## Ability Score Abbreviations

These six stats define your character. Each has a **modifier** derived from the score: 10–11 = +0, 12–13 = +1, 8–9 = –1, and so on.

| Abbr | Stat | Used For |
| ---- | ---- | -------- |
| **STR** | Strength | Wagon repair, river crossings, pushing wagons uphill |
| **DEX** | Dexterity | Hunting, navigating treacherous terrain, quick reactions |
| **CON** | Constitution | Resisting illness, enduring cold, surviving injuries |
| **INT** | Intelligence | Diagnosing disease, navigation, bartering |
| **WIS** | Wisdom | Trail sense, weather reading, finding shelter |
| **CHA** | Charisma | Fort negotiation, managing morale, diplomacy |

## Core Mechanical Terms

**Ability Check (Skill Check)**
The fundamental action resolution: roll **d20 + modifier** and compare to a target number (the DC). If you meet or exceed the DC, you succeed.

**DC (Difficulty Class)**
The target number for a check. Higher = harder. The scale runs from 6 (Easy) to 23 (Near Impossible).

**Modifier**
The bonus or penalty added to a d20 roll, derived from an ability score. A score of 14–15 gives a +2 modifier; a score of 6–7 gives a –2.

**Advantage**
Roll **2d20 and take the higher result.** A significant benefit granted by favorable circumstances, good equipment, or allies helping.

**Disadvantage**
Roll **2d20 and take the lower result.** A significant penalty from exhaustion, illness, bad conditions, or low morale. If you have both Advantage and Disadvantage, they cancel out and you roll normally.

**Simple Check**
One character rolls once. Succeed or fail, move on.

**Group Check**
Everyone in the party rolls. If more than half succeed, the group succeeds. Used for river crossings, mountain passes, blizzards.

**Extended Check**
A multi-round challenge requiring several successes before accumulating too many failures (e.g., "3 successes before 2 failures"). Used for complex tasks like lowering a wagon down a cliff or negotiating with a Native band.

**Contested Check**
Your roll vs. the GM's roll. Whoever rolls higher wins. Used for bartering and detecting ambushes.

**Natural 1**
Rolling a 1 on the d20 — the worst possible result. In this game, it triggers a Critical Failure during fort negotiations.

**Saving Throw**
A D&D term for a check made to resist something harmful. In this system there is no mechanical difference between a "save" and a "check" — the terms are interchangeable.

**Inspiration**
A one-use token that grants Advantage on a single check of your choice (declared before rolling). You can hold at most 1 at a time. Earned through Evening Activities during rest.

## General TTRPG Terms

**GM (Game Master)**
The person who runs the game — describes the world, controls events, plays NPCs, and adjudicates rules. Everyone else is a "player."

**NPC (Non-Player Character)**
Any character controlled by the GM rather than a player (fort traders, Native leaders, other emigrants, etc.).

**Party**
The group of player characters traveling together.

**RPG / TTRPG**
Role-Playing Game / Tabletop Role-Playing Game. A game where players take on character roles and resolve actions through rules and dice.

**D&D / 5e**
Dungeons & Dragons, Fifth Edition — the tabletop RPG whose core resolution mechanic (d20 + modifier vs. DC) this game borrows. This game does **not** use D&D's classes, levels, proficiency bonus, AC, or combat rounds.

**SRD (Systems Reference Document)**
The publicly licensed portion of the 5e rules that other games can legally build on.

**HP (Hit Points)**
A number representing health or structural integrity. Characters, the wagon, and ox teams all have HP. At 0 HP, bad things happen.

**Solo Play**
One person playing all four party roles and serving as GM simultaneously.

**Roleplay**
Acting in character — making decisions, speaking dialogue, and reacting as your character would, as distinct from rolling dice.

**Action Economy**
How many things can be done in a given time period. In this game, the wagon moves as a single unit — if one character hunts, it affects everyone's travel.

**Party Wipe**
All party members dying. Game over.

**Point Buy**
A character-creation method (from D&D) where players allocate points among stats. Explicitly not used here — you roll 3d6 in order and live with it.

**Face Character**
Informal TTRPG jargon for whichever party member handles social/diplomatic situations (the best CHA roller).

**Farming**
Exploiting a mechanic repeatedly — here, killing characters to get "fresh" replacements without accumulated exhaustion or disease. The Ghost Tax exists to prevent this.

**Safety Tools**
Table-level agreements and mechanisms (like the X-Card, Lines & Veils, or Script Change) that let any player pause or redirect play if content becomes uncomfortable.

## Oregon Trail RPG — Game-Specific Terms

### Trail Structure

**Trail Day**
The fundamental unit of play — one in-game day. Follows a structured loop: Weather, Stance, Event, Action, Resources, Camp.

**Segment**
One of seven major geographic phases of the journey (e.g., Segment 1: The Plains, miles 0–300). Each has its own event table and hazard profile.

**Mile Marker**
The party's current position on the trail, measured in miles from Independence (0) to the Willamette Valley (~2,000).

**Montage (Pushing Forward)**
A fast-forward option: instead of playing 3 individual Trail Days, the party banks 3 days of Standard travel progress and rolls only one Daily Event. Available when weather is non-hazardous.

**Time Loss (Lost Days)**
When events cost the party calendar days with no miles gained. Resources are still consumed; weather still shifts.

**Event Table / Universal Event Table**
The d20 table the GM rolls each Trail Day. Results 1–9 can be swapped for segment-specific 1d10 events. Results 10–20 are universal (mix of neutral and positive).

### Travel

**Travel Stance**
The party's daily pace choice: **Cautious** (8–10 mi/day, safer), **Standard** (15–20 mi/day), or **Reckless** (22–30 mi/day, dangerous).

**Hard Travel**
Any day the party travels Recklessly or attempts a Forced March. Doubles food consumption and strains oxen.

**Forced March**
Pushing 50% beyond normal stance distance. Requires a group CON check (DC 11); failures gain Exhaustion. Cannot combine with Reckless stance.

**Travel Check**
A group WIS or CON check triggered only when a specific hazard demands it. Failure = half distance + the hazard's consequence.

**Navigation Check**
A WIS check (usually by the Guide) to stay on the correct trail, pick the right fork, or find the safest crossing.

### Characters

**Role**
One of four party positions: **Guide** (WIS), **Doctor** (INT), **Hunter** (DEX), **Teamster** (STR). Each has a primary stat, secondary stat, and Competence Floor ability.

**Background Bump**
An optional modifier applied after stat rolling: +2 to primary stat, +1 to secondary stat.

**Background Hook**
A single mechanical benefit chosen during character creation from a role-specific list (e.g., Sharpshooter, Frontier Herbalist).

**Competence Floor**
Each role's built-in safety net — a special ability that prevents catastrophic failure at the role's core job:
- **Take 10** (Guide): Substitute 10 for the d20 on easy navigation checks
- **Jury-Rig** (Teamster): Spend 1 max Wagon HP to reroll a failed repair
- **Take Aim** (Hunter): Spend a full day to guarantee 50 lbs food, no ammo cost
- **Triage** (Doctor): Spend an extra medicine dose for Advantage on a treatment check

**Wound Track**
The death-avoidance system. At 0 HP you don't die immediately — you move through stages: **Wounded** (Disadvantage on STR/DEX) → **Gravely Ill / Injured** (bedridden) → **Dead** (permanent). Healing HP does *not* automatically clear your Wound Track state.

**Bedridden**
A character who is Gravely Ill/Injured or at Exhaustion level 5. Cannot take actions; counts as ~150 lbs of cargo.

**Exhaustion**
A tiered condition (levels 1–6) that progressively degrades a character. Level 1 = Disadvantage on all checks. Level 6 = death. Removed by Rest Days and food.

**Exhaustion Cap**
At most 1 involuntary Exhaustion level per day (from failed checks or hazards). Voluntary sources (double watch shifts) can exceed this.

**Ghost Tax**
The mechanical penalties when a replacement character joins after a death: **Integration Cost** (50 lbs food, 2 medicine, or $20), **Shared Burden** (1 Exhaustion), and **Role Lockout** (can't use the dead character's Competence Floor for 3 trail days).

**Secondary Survivor**
The preferred type of replacement character — a family member or companion who was already in the wagon as a background NPC.

**Inherited Burden**
When a stranger replaces a dead character, the deceased extracts a promise. The new character's Morale is tethered to fulfilling it.

### Resources

**Morale**
A party-wide score (0–10, starts at 7). High Morale (8–10) grants Advantage on group checks. Low Morale (1–2) imposes Disadvantage on everything. At 0, the party disbands.

**Wagon HP**
The wagon's structural integrity (max 30). At 0, the wagon is unusable — cache supplies and walk.

**Spare Parts**
Discrete wagon components (wheels, axles, tongues). Required for standard field repairs. Extremely valuable for barter at western forts.

**Medicine (doses)**
Consumable resource required for the Doctor to treat serious illness. Spent whether treatment succeeds or fails.

**Ammunition (rounds)**
Consumed by hunting: 10 rounds per attempt, even on failure.

**Oxen / Ox Teams**
Draft animals (10 HP per team). Each lost team reduces travel speed by 25%. At 0 teams: stranded.

**Wagon Capacity**
Maximum cargo: 2,500 lbs. Rarely relevant except when events force supply drops or bedridden passengers add weight.

**Warm Bedding**
Buffalo robes / heavy blankets. Characters count as Sheltered for nighttime cold damage. Does not protect oxen or the wagon. Lost if the wagon is destroyed or supplies are dumped.

**Sheltered**
A condition meaning the party has physical protection from weather (at a fort, in dense trees, canyon, etc.). Can be achieved via a WIS (Survival) check if no natural cover exists.

**Pemmican**
A special food item available only through successful Native Encounter trading. Lasts forever and counts as 1.5x food value.

**Tonics**
Created by the Doctor's Brew Tonics camp action. Drinking one grants Advantage on the next CON check vs. disease.

### Rest & Camp

**Rest Day (Layover)**
A full day with no travel. The party heals 1d4+1 HP, removes 1 Exhaustion, oxen recover 1 HP, and each character takes one Camp Action. Food consumption is halved (0.5 lbs/person).

**Camp Action**
One of seven activities a character can perform during a Rest Day: Deep Repair, Preventative Maintenance, Foraging/Fishing, Intensive Care, Brew Tonics, Scouting Ahead, or Mend & Wash.

**Teamster's Upkeep**
An automatic Rest Day benefit for the Teamster: restore 1 Wagon HP with no check or parts.

**Evening Activity**
An optional once-per-layover group activity (campfire stories, journaling, or a proper hot meal) that can boost Morale or grant Inspiration.

### Encounters & Trading

**Sutler**
A historical term for the civilian store owner at a military fort or trading post. They set the prices and know you have nowhere else to go.

**Price Multiplier**
The escalating cost factor at each fort: 1.5x at Fort Kearny up to 4x at Fort Boise.

**Sutler's Demand**
A specific item or piece of information the Sutler wants. Meeting it reduces the Negotiation DC by 5.

**Standoff State**
An escalated state during a Native Encounter (reached after 2 failed checks). The party makes a final group CHA or WIS check (DC 12) to de-escalate.

**Crisis**
The most dangerous outcome in a Native Encounter — triggered by hostility or failing a Standoff. The party must surrender double goods or risk combat.

**Cultural Broker**
The Guide's special ability in Native Encounters: once per encounter, automatically negate one failed check.

### Route Variants

**Sublette Cutoff**
A shortcut bypassing Fort Bridger. Saves 70 miles but crosses a waterless desert requiring active water tracking.

**Hastings Cutoff**
A historically fatal route (the Donner Party took it in 1846). Adds 14 days, forces 10 days of worst-case events, imposes Disadvantage on all checks.

**Barlow Road**
A steep toll road over Mount Hood. The "safe" finale option — if you can pay and your wagon survives the descent.

**Columbia River Route**
The rafting finale. Dismantle the wagon, build a raft, navigate rapids. High stakes, no wagon on the other side.

**Lost State**
A special game state entered during the Hastings Cutoff where the party is mechanically lost for 14 days.
