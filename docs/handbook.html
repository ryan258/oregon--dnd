Oregon Trail RPG — Start playing

Version 0.9 playtest edition. Four ordinary people, one wagon, and two thousand miles of decisions nobody gets to take back. This page gets you playing in five minutes; core rules settle shared mechanics.

What are we willing to spend, and what do we refuse to lose, to get these people to Oregon?

Everything in this game is an answer to that question. The food barrel is an answer. The day you stop to help a stranger is an answer. So is the day you don't.

Start in five minutes

  1. Pick a GM and distribute the four pregens. With fewer than four players, share the extra travelers. No D&D book needed.
  2. Agree tone and boundaries; read Dream, Promise, Boundary, and Bond aloud. Revise with the other players' agreement.
  3. For tonight, open The River Between and use its exact starting state. For the full journey use the starter loadout below and Seven Chapters.
  4. Put the route map, wagon sheet, GM reference, and people ledger within reach.
  5. State the visible problem and at least two options. Make the first decision before teaching every subsystem.

The four travelers

Four jobs, four kinds of authority, four ways to be the one who says wait. Each has work that simply succeeds, and one intervention that costs real resources to buy certainty when it matters.

Role Dependable contribution Costly intervention
Guide Routine marked-route navigation DC 12 or lower succeeds unless obscured. One scouting day reveals both routes' real immediate costs/hazards.
Doctor Identify a condition and its risks without rolling. On Rest Day, 1 medicine + Camp Action/daily treatment prevents patient's condition damage today and removes 2 marks from one condition instead of its check.
Hunter A full Hunt Day + 5 ammunition gives 30 lbs fresh food where game exists. Once/chapter, halve the four travelers' next three days of food; cannot feed from zero.
Teamster +1 Wagon HP on Rest Days without action. One part + Repair Day/Camp Action restores 6 Wagon HP guaranteed.

New characters use scores 15, 13, 12, 10, 10, 8 assigned freely and HP 8 + CON modifier. Choose one background. No Background Bump. Pregens have all this filled in.

Roll only for an uncertain consequence

Nobody rolls to find out what a thing costs. The price goes on the table first; the dice only decide whether you paid it.

GM states ability, DC, success and failure before commitment. Roll d20 + modifier, meeting DC succeeds. DC 10 ordinary, 12 difficult, 14 dangerous. Advantage: higher of two dice; Disadvantage: lower. They cancel unless Exhaustion 3+. No stacking. Helping can grant Advantage if the helper shares the risk.

Group check: every able active traveler rolls. More than half succeed = full success; at least one but not a majority = partial; none = failure. Use printed outcomes. Without printed partial: complete the immediate goal, halve numeric damage/loss rounding up, keep delays. Incapacitated people do not roll. A natural 1/20 has no automatic result.

No check forces a PC's belief, departure, or obedience. No failed bargain creates automatic violence.

Character decisions matter

The wagon is shared. The reasons for being in it are not — and the game pays you for letting that difference cost you something.

Once per character per session, a meaningful cost/complication arising from Dream, Promise, Bond, or Boundary earns Inspiration. Hold one, spend before a check for Advantage. Acting out dialogue is optional; explain your intent plainly.

At chapter end, affirm or revise a personal statement. After chapters 2 and 5 choose one new technique. Record specific NPC promises/favors, including what they can actually provide. They matter at the finish.

Travel

Most days are not stories. Batch them, spend the food, and slow down when the road puts a real question in front of somebody.

Use three-day legs between major scenes: choose stance, roll one event for the leg, apply each day's travel/supplies/weather/conditions in batches. Weather can be hazardous; it does not prohibit batching. Stop before advancing unused days if a decision or medical crisis appears. A prepared scene replaces that day's random event. Fort days and days lost to an existing event do not add random events. Do not charge a failed event day twice.

Stance Miles/day before modifiers Food/person Event
Cautious 10 1 lb Two complete events; players choose after warnings.
Standard 18 1 lb One event.
Reckless 26 2 lbs Two complete events; GM chooses the riskier and explains.

Two or more usable ox teams: full speed; one: ×0.75; none: no wagon movement. At least two travelers at Exhaustion 2+: party speed ×0.5 once. Fewer than two able travelers: wagon travel stops. Apply weather and scene multipliers, round miles upward once at the end. Hard travel costs one team 1 HP/day, or 2 in a heatwave. Forced March is optional; see core before using it.

Event selector d6: 1–3 chapter event d6; 4 recurring person; 5 opportunity (20 lbs cured food, sheltered site, or reliable report); 6 quiet progress. No Universal d20 event table. After two costly random events in succession, next random event becomes a quiet opportunity/message.

A day's order

Reveal weather/danger → choose action/stance → event if due → resolve action → pay food/water and effects → condition recovery → Rest recovery if applicable → watch/conversation → advance date.

Weather changes every three calendar days and at chapter entry. Weather table. Rest/Hunt/Repair/Trade stop travel. Hunt while traveling is allowed at half miles, normal hunt check/cost.

Supplies and rest

  • Ordinary four-person day: 4 lbs food. Hard travel: 8. Rest: 2. Missing a full ration gives that traveler 1 Exhaustion. No penalty merely for a low reserve while everyone is fed.
  • Track water only in waterless stretches: 2 gallons/day for party and teams, 4 in heat. Insufficient daily amount: each traveler loses 1 HP and gains 1 Exhaustion; one team loses 2 HP. Reaching zero after paying the full amount is not yet deprivation.
  • Fresh meat: 100-lb cap, expires after the third day following acquisition. Mark date; preserve on a Rest Day. Cured food keeps unless a warned event threatens it.
  • Hunt: 5 ammunition, DEX DC 10 good terrain / 14 poor; success 60 / 30 lbs fresh food. Take Aim is 30 guaranteed with full Hunt Day and 5 ammunition. Check availability first.
  • Wagon max 30 HP; at 0 cannot move but can be repaired. Field Repair STR DC 10, day/action, 1d4+2 HP capped 15; on success spend a part for 1d6+3 instead, up to max. One repair action/day, plus upkeep.
  • Rest: fed travelers heal 1d4+1 HP, remove 1 Exhaustion, and may recover one wound step with CON DC 10. Each living team +1 HP. Each able traveler gets one Camp Action; Teamster upkeep is free. Conversation is available every night.

Wagon category limits: 800 lbs food total (including fresh), 10 medicine, 200 ammo, 5 parts, 80 gallons water capacity, two personal cargo bundles, and one entrusted bundle. Fresh food also has its own 100-lb cap. After two successful hunts at one campsite, move at least 18 actual miles before further hunting there.

Harm without excluding a player

People get hurt on this road and some of them die on it. No player ever sits and watches because of it.

At first reaching 0 HP: Wounded. Later separate damage at 0 or returning to 0: Gravely Injured; another such instance: Dead. One hit advances only one step. At 0 HP physical actions are unavailable; Gravely Injured allows no checks/actions. Ordinary stabilization uses an able traveler's action to restore 1 HP. HP healing alone does not clear wounds.

Exhaustion: 1 = −1 checks; 2 = physical Disadvantage replaces −1; 3 = all checks Disadvantage that Advantage cannot cancel; 4 = max HP halved; 5 = incapacitated; 6 = death. At most one new Exhaustion per day, all sources. Removal requires full ration. Use core for condition recovery; recovery rolls ignore condition/Exhaustion/Morale penalties.

The player immediately controls a reserve or shares another active traveler while incapacitated. Four active slots maximum; extra bodies need explicit food accounting. After death introduce a replacement immediately, normal full HP, Exhaustion 1, current techniques, no free supplies, no fee/ability lockout. Mark the grave and a memory.

Morale and people

Morale is the group's shared answer to was this worth leaving for? It falls when you lose someone or break your word, and it rises at a landmark, a kept promise, or one honest evening at the fire.

Start 7, range 0–10. At 8+, Advantage on one group check/session. At 3–4, a camp disagreement; at 1–2, a breaking-point conversation. At 0, reach safe camp and choose to spend a Rest Day regrouping to 3 or agree to end the expedition. No automatic desertion.

−1: death, known broken significant NPC promise, crossing below 50 lbs food or 5 Wagon HP (each threshold only once/chapter). +1: first arrival at chapter endpoint, significant promise fulfilled once, or one Rest evening activity. No duplicate landmark rewards.

Campaign starter purchases

April 20; marker 0; Morale 7; wagon 30 HP; three teams 10 HP each. $800 buys 700 lbs cured food, 3 teams, 7 medicine, 3 parts, 160 ammunition, 4 extra barrels, Warm Bedding for $550, leaving $250. Included wagon/tools and base water plus barrels give 60 gallons capacity, filled. This is fifteen hot-weather days, not a survival guarantee. River standalone uses its own snapshot.

Tonight's ending

End on a question, not a cliff. The best stopping point is the moment before somebody has to choose.

Stop at a real decision, record state in the trail log, and ask what each traveler has not yet said. If the expedition ends, use Arrival. Read the worked examples only when helpful; play does not require memorizing them.

The River Between

Ten dollars, chalked on a board. A ferryman who charges everyone the same. A schoolteacher counting coins on a crate of books, coming up short. And a ford upstream that will take you across today, for free, for a price.

Nothing in this chapter is hidden. Every route is quoted openly in the first ten minutes: the safe one, the slow one, and the fast one. What the party is actually deciding is whether a stranger's problem is theirs — and what they are willing to stake on the answer. There is no wrong crossing here. There is only the one they choose and then have to live beside on the far bank.

A complete introductory chapter for Oregon Trail RPG v0.9. One GM, 1–4 players controlling four travelers, target 2–3 hours. This duration is untested. Use the pregens, reference, wagon sheet, and people ledger. All people and local scene sites are fictional.

Five-minute start

Put the three options on paper before you teach a single rule. The party should be arguing about the river inside ten minutes.

Give everyone a character. Read their Dreams and Promises aloud, ask agreement on Bonds, and allow changes. State the game promise: consequences are real; risks are disclosed; everyone keeps playing. Confirm boundaries using session zero. Put three options on paper: ferry, upper ford, wait/help.

Standalone start: June 2, marker 340, Morale 6; 140 lbs cured food, no fresh food, 60 gallons capacity/full (water not tracked here), 4 medicine, 40 ammunition, 2 parts, $25, Warm Bedding, tools, wagon 22/30, three teams at 8/10 each. Use pregens' full HP, Exhaustion 0, one Inspiration. A crate of Eliza's books and Mae's tools are named personal cargo bundles; no other cargo inventory is needed. These replace the campaign's starting purchases, not additional goods.

Campaign entry uses actual state and starts the scene when the party reaches 340. If the party arrives in a different month, use actual weather. For the standalone, first three days are Clear and Mild; roll weather beginning June 5. Subsequent weather follows the core rules.

Three locations

One bend of one river, and three ways to be on the other side of it. Scouting, waiting, and working are all real uses of a day here — none of them move the marker.

340 — QUEUE CAMP: names, needs, accurate prices, safe Rest possible
         | one local scouting day; no route-marker progress
         +—— UPPER FORD: inspect it before choosing; return to camp free
         |
         +—— FERRY LANDING: loading work and payment
                   |
                   +—— far-bank marker 358 — road onward to Laramie 500

These are alternative crossing approaches in the same local area. A completed crossing uses one Travel Day and advances 18 miles to 358, regardless of stance; no extra random event, stock fee, or travel check. Scouting, queue days, Rest, and loading labor do not advance the route marker. Weather river DC modifiers apply to the ford. Ferry remains safe in ordinary showers; in a thunderstorm or blizzard the operator waits, disclosing one weather span's delay. Waiting days may be Rest Days.

Scene 1 — A price everyone can see

Read or paraphrase: “The crossing rope hums in the current. On a board beside the landing, somebody has written TEN DOLLARS. Mercy Reed is counting coins beside an open crate of books. The operator points to the same price for everyone.”

Operator Ellis Ward: wants to clear the queue safely; refuses overloaded runs; no secret hostility. Mercy: $10 short, owns books she can sell, refuses to leave her brother behind. Tomas: can help build a loading ramp but needs another able worker. Nessa: leaving for Laramie after her freight crosses, can carry a message for $1.

The party hears all three routes for free:

  • Ferry: $10 party total; next departure tomorrow. Today can be a Rest Day. Guaranteed safe crossing, tools/cargo/teams included. Another $10 pays Mercy's passage. No social roll to pay.
  • Work: spend today repairing the loading ramp. Two able PCs commit their actions; the other two may Rest individually. Tomorrow both the party and Mercy cross for no cash. Workers eat 1 lb each and receive no Rest recovery; resting travelers eat 0.5 lb each and get their own normal recovery/actions. Wagon upkeep applies if the Teamster rests; oxen rest because the wagon does not move. This is the scene's explicit mixed-day exception.
  • Upper ford: can cross today, dangerous; exact outcomes below. Scouting first costs one day and gives Advantage to the crossing group check. It cannot be combined with ramp labor by the same person. Guide Read the ground guarantees the information already given plus Advantage here; do not charge twice for discovering visible stakes.

No one forces a charitable choice. If refused, Mercy sells books to cross later. The consequences are her changed prospects and the absence of a favor, not a moral penalty imposed by the GM.

Scene 2 — The crossing

Whatever they chose, make the crossing itself a scene about hands and cargo, not about dice. Ask what each person carries across personally.

For ferry/work, ask each traveler what they insist on unloading themselves. Then cross safely; no hidden roll after payment. Give every player a voice without manufacturing danger to punish the safe option.

For upper ford, name the current before asking for commitment. Choose a dependable rope line: spend 1 part (represents anchors) and one extra preparation day, then cross safely. Or group DEX/WIS DC 12 (each player chooses based on their described contribution):

Outcome Result
Full: more than half succeed Everyone and all cargo cross. No damage.
Partial: at least one succeeds, not a majority Cross, wagon loses 2 HP. Players choose either one lost cargo bundle or one volunteering traveler takes 1d4 HP damage protecting it.
Failure: none succeed Retreat to this bank; no crossing progress. Wagon loses 4 HP and one team loses 2 HP. Tomorrow the ferry, ramp work, and rope-line plan remain available.

Scouting gives Advantage; a helper cannot add more. A personal-stakes Inspiration can benefit its owner's check but not stack. One failed participant alone does not drown. Apply Wound rules if damage reaches 0 HP.

Every role has a decision: Guide chooses information versus time; Teamster decides whether the part is worth a guaranteed crossing; Doctor reserves medicine for a patient or spends their day's effort helping; Hunter weighs waiting against a hunt. A Hunter who hunts on a waiting day cannot also repair the ramp or Rest. No game is guaranteed at the queue; nearby game is available in the standalone, so Take Aim is a valid alternative.

Scene 3 — What crosses with you

The far bank is quieter than anyone expects. Wet rope, a fire, and the first real chance to count what came over with them.

At the first far-bank camp, each player answers or passes: “What did you hand to somebody else because you trusted them more than the river?” A fulfilled significant promise grants its one Morale. If helping Mercy cost time or goods, award the involved character's Inspiration if eligible.

Mercy gives her accurate river report if helped. Record river intelligence: lower one finale raft-run DC to 10. This same report meets Laramie's demand; using it there does not destroy the knowledge. Tomas does not also grant a repair favor merely for being present; that requires his chapter 3 help.

If a personal bundle was lost, its owner decides what the loss means for their dream. A tool chest can be replaced by a new promise to work for tools; a lost book crate does not erase the character's reason to continue.

Finishing the chapter

Eight ordinary days of road to Fort Laramie. This is the group's first taste of the campaign's real rhythm: batch the miles, spend the food, stop when something asks a question.

From far-bank marker 358 to Laramie 500 is 142 miles: eight ordinary 18-mile travel days, stopping at 500 rather than overshooting. Use two three-day legs and one two-day leg, with one event per leg; short final legs still receive one event. On chapter 2 event-table results that would introduce another major crossing, reinterpret the site as a minor side-channel with the same disclosed mechanics, or replace it openly with a traveler message before rolling. Do not force an extra major set piece just to fill time.

For a shorter first session, end at the far-bank camp and record state. That is a complete introductory adventure; continue the remaining road in the next session. Do not award Laramie arrival or chapter advancement before reaching it.

At Laramie, endpoint Morale +1, trade/rest if desired, and one technique per PC. Owen Bell accepts an accurate crossing report for his demand. Use the worked trade if needed. Finish by changing or affirming a personal statement and naming one person to see again.

Six optional complications

Use the six chapter 2 events in Events for the onward road. During the three prepared scenes, use no random event. If the queue needs texture, describe a dropped cup, a repair argument, or another wagon's arrival without charging a second surprise hazard.

GM answers and recovery

Every failure state here has a printed way onward. Nobody gets stranded at 340 because the dice were unkind or the purse was empty.

  • No cash or parts? Ramp labor still buys safe passage; willing labor is sufficient, no roll. If fewer than two PCs can work, Tomas supplies the second worker and adds one extra labor day. If none can work, Mercy sells books to fund passage, asking an explicit future favor; no inactive player is left waiting for a solvency check.
  • Two injured PCs? Use reserve travelers immediately. Count active slots and food explicitly. Tomas can care for a patient with his party while the reserve occupies the active slot, if agreed.
  • Failed ford? The party stays at 340 and pays that failed day once. No endless downstream rescue maze. The other approaches remain.
  • Party splits over payment? Give each player one statement of priorities, then agree the shared wagon's plan. No CHA roll overrides a player.
  • Players invent a winch, raft, or barter? Give the relevant person a credible reason to agree or decline, then state a time/material cost and DC before commitment. Equivalent preparation should buy equivalent safety.

Debrief

Use Playtest. Ask which choice felt real, whether every role mattered, and which person they want to meet again. Record actual elapsed time and rules lookups; do not rate the game for the players.

GM reference — v0.9

Warn them. Offer a choice. Name the price. Then carry it forward.

Authority: core. Start: river chapter. Events: chapter d6. Weather: seasonal d6.

Table contract

Warn → offer choices → state DC/cost → roll if uncertain → carry the consequence forward. Reliable costly plans work. No roll controls a PC. Every player keeps an active role through incapacity/death. Two consecutive costly random events → next random event is quiet opportunity/message.

Checks

D20 + modifier vs DC 10 ordinary / 12 difficult / 14 dangerous. Advantage/Disadvantage cancel, never stack; Exhaustion 3+ Disadvantage cannot cancel. Group: more than half full; 1+ but no majority partial; none failure. Incapacitated people excluded. Printed outcomes first; fallback partial completes goal, halves numeric damage/loss upward, full delays. Extended: 3 successes before 2 failures, varied contributors, stated outcomes.

Time and travel

Default leg: 3 days, one event on first day. Short leg to marker allowed, still one event. Daily weather/health/costs remain. Interrupt before spending unused days. Prepared scene replaces event; fort/delay days no extra events. Daily: reveal → choose → event → action → costs/effects → condition recovery → Rest recovery → watch/camp → date.

Cautious 10 miles (players choose milder of two events); Standard 18; Reckless 26 (GM chooses riskier of two). Two usable teams full; one ×0.75; zero stopped. Two travelers at Exhaustion 2+ multiply ×0.5 once; fewer than two able travelers stops wagon. Apply weather, round once upward. Forced March Cautious/Standard: group CON DC 12, full +50%, partial +25%/failed rollers +1 Exhaustion, failure no bonus/all rollers +1. All attempts hard travel. Hard travel: 1 team loses 1 HP (2 in heatwave).

Event selector d6: 1–3 chapter d6; 4 recurring person; 5 opportunity; 6 quiet. Weather changes every 3 calendar days and chapter entry. Shelter WIS DC 10 / 12 exposed terrain; failed search can become full-day guaranteed shelter-building. Bedding protects travelers from cold, not stock.

Resources

Food/person: ordinary 1 lb, hard 2, Rest 0.5. Missing full ration +1 Exhaustion; no food at Exhaustion 3+ also 1 HP. Water/party in dry stretches 2 gal or 4 in heat; insufficient day's amount each traveler −1 HP/+1 Exhaustion, one team −2 HP. Fresh cap 100 lbs; expiry acquisition +3 days after evening; cured stores keep.

Hunt full day or half-speed travel: 5 ammo, DEX DC 10/14, yield 60/30 fresh lbs. Take Aim full Hunt Day, 5 ammo, 30 guaranteed with game. Repair STR DC 10: 1d4+2 capped 15, or on success part for 1d6+3 up to 30. Teamster Jury-rig part for 6 guaranteed. One repair/day + Rest upkeep 1. Cargo bundle: 50 food / 1 part / 40 ammo / filled extra barrel / named personal cargo.

Harm and recovery

At first 0 HP Wounded; separate hit at 0 or return to 0 advances Gravely then Dead. One step/hit. Ordinary stabilization action restores 1 HP. Wounded: physical tasks unavailable at 0, STR/DEX Disadvantage otherwise. Gravely: no actions/checks, can ride. Oregon Trail RPG — Core Rules

Version 0.9 — September 2026 playtest edition. A complete campaign for one GM and 1–4 players controlling four travelers. Seven chapters, usually 6–8 sessions of 2–3 hours; that duration is a design target, not measured playtest evidence. For a single session use The River Between.

What are we willing to spend, and what do we refuse to lose, to get these people to Oregon?

Everything you own is in one wagon. It weighs too much. It has always weighed too much, and the road will eventually make you say out loud which part of it you were lying to yourself about.

You are ordinary people taking one wagon west in 1848. The destination is shared; your reasons are not. Time, supplies, weather, obligations, and the people you meet shape what arrival means. Nothing here is free: a day spent helping a stranger is a day of food, a day of weather, a day closer to the snow. That is the game. Not whether you survive — what you are still carrying when you do.

Death is permanent. Preparation matters. Every player keeps playing.

This is historical fiction, not a historical or medical simulation. Mile markers, prices, water quantities, disease effects, and named scenarios are game abstractions. The story takes place on inhabited Indigenous lands; arrival does not establish a moral or legal entitlement to land. See historical framing and sources.

Start and authority

Read the quickstart, use the pregenerated party, and put the route map and wagon sheet on the table. Agree on tone and boundaries. The campaign supplies seven playable chapter frames.

This file is authoritative for shared mechanics. Numbered modules supply procedures and content; an explicitly stated local exception overrides the core for that scene only. Quickstart, glossary, examples, and sheets summarize rather than create rules. No external D&D rulebook is required. This game uses six ability scores and d20 checks, but no classes, levels, armor class, proficiency, or tactical combat rounds.

1. People before purchases

Before anyone asks how much flour to buy, ask what they would not sell it for.

The party always has four active travelers. With 2–3 players, share the extra sheets and rotate their decisions; with one player use solo rules. The four roles are Guide, Doctor, Hunter, and Teamster. One player can delegate bookkeeping without delegating their character's decisions. Supporting people encountered along the way are separate parties with their own supplies unless explicitly taken aboard; the default game never silently adds dependents or their consumption.

Assign scores 15, 13, 12, 10, 10, 8 freely. Put 15 in your role's primary ability if you want dependable starting expertise. Modifiers: 8–9 = −1; 10–11 = +0; 12–13 = +1; 14–15 = +2; 16–17 = +3; 18 = +4. STR is labor; DEX precision; CON endurance; INT analysis; WIS observation; CHA negotiation. No scores grow automatically.

Optional harsh creation: roll 3d6 in order, then swap any two scores. Only use this with everyone's agreement; the fixed array is the default. Do not add the former Background Bump to either method.

Choose one background. Start with 8 + CON modifier HP, no wounds, Exhaustion 0, and one Inspiration. Record:

  • Dream: I am going to Oregon because… Give it a concrete person, place, or undertaking.
  • Promise: I promised [person] that I would… Another PC or recurring NPC should be involved.
  • Boundary: I will not sacrifice… Choose something that can enter a decision, not a command controlling another player.
  • Bond: I rely on [PC] for…, and they know this about me… Ask that player's agreement.
  • Keepsake: one small object connected to the above. No weight or monetary value unless agreed.

Each player names one possible reserve traveler in another wagon party. They are fictional characters, not extra free labor or supplies. Use one if that player's main character becomes incapacitated or dies.

2. Checks, risks, and permission to choose

The dice are not the danger. The danger was disclosed before anyone picked them up.

Roll d20 + ability modifier, meeting or exceeding the DC to succeed. Advantage rolls two d20 and keeps the higher; Disadvantage keeps the lower. Multiple sources never stack. Both together cancel, except Exhaustion 3+. A natural 1 or 20 has no automatic effect unless an entry explicitly says so.

DC Meaning
8 Favorable but consequential
10 Uncertain ordinary task
12 Difficult under pressure
14 Dangerous expert work
16 Exceptional danger; preparation strongly advised

Do not roll when failure changes nothing. Do not call an impossible task a high DC: name the equipment, time, help, or changed plan that would make it possible.

Before a roll, state the stakes, ability, DC, and failure cost. For major danger offer at least two approaches, including a dependable costly option where plausible. Players can propose another approach. Fix its stakes before rolling. Information plainly visible is free; scouting reveals hidden facts rather than permission to know the rules.

  • Simple: one character rolls. One helper with a relevant action can grant Advantage, but shares the named risk. No repeated rolls for the same approach without changing conditions or paying its stated retry cost.
  • Group: each able participant rolls; incapacitated travelers are passengers and do not roll. More than half succeeding is full success. At least one success without a majority is a partial success. No successes is failure. With four rollers, 3–4 / 1–2 / 0 are the three outcomes. Use the scene's printed results. If unspecified: partial means complete the immediate goal and take half listed numeric damage/loss, rounded up; apply listed delays in full. Failure means no immediate progress plus the listed consequence. No invented extra death or injury.
  • Extended: 3 successes before 2 failures. Different approaches or contributors must be used where plausible; no character rolls twice consecutively if another can act. State all outcomes before starting. On success achieve the task. On failure use the printed setback; no automatic escalation to violence. Ordinary buying, conversation, or fair agreements do not require an extended check.

Conflicts between travelers: roleplay or summarize, then players decide their own characters' responses. CHA never compels another PC to obey. Violence among PCs requires prior player agreement and can always be excluded.

Daily procedure

  1. Reveal prevailing weather, visible danger, and any time limit. Weather changes every three calendar days and at a chapter boundary; reroll there and restart the three-day count.
  2. Choose Travel, Hunt, Repair, Trade, or Rest. For Travel choose a stance. A known threat can be met by changing the action before rolling.
  3. Generate an event if due (see travel legs). Present the warning and choices, resolve it, then the chosen action if compatible. A scene replacing that day's event never adds a second event.
  4. Pay that day's food and water; apply travel strain, weather, and ongoing condition effects. Treat before condition damage when a treatment is declared that day.
  5. Make condition recovery checks; on Rest Days apply healing, Exhaustion recovery, and Wound recovery. Set three watch shifts and play or summarize a camp conversation. Advance the date once.

Days lost to waiting, detours, weather, or failed repairs still consume supplies, progress conditions, and advance weather. They do not generate additional random events. Say whether the delay permits Rest; waiting for a ferry does, physical labor or backtracking does not. Count an event's stated delay as replacing that day's action unless it explicitly says additional days. Never charge the event day twice.

Travel legs: skip repeated decisions

The default between scenes is a three-day travel leg, at one chosen stance. A planned leg ending at a scene or marker can last one or two days; it still gets one event unless the prepared scene replaces it. Roll one event for the whole leg, occurring on its first day, instead of three daily events. Apply each day's mileage, consumption, weather, fresh-food expiry, and condition checks; batch identical arithmetic. Weather can change inside a leg; use the remaining days of the old weather, then roll the new weather. Harmful weather does not prohibit a leg.

Use a single Focus Day for a known obstacle, a condition likely to incapacitate someone that day, a fort, a route choice, or a player-requested scene. Finish the current day when a leg reveals such an obstacle, then pause before advancing more time. The unused days are unspent: do not charge them or award their miles. Resume with a new leg after the scene. Do not reroll the same day's event.

A Focus Day chosen solely to magnify conversation during an otherwise ordinary leg does not add an event. A standalone day outside a leg gets one event, unless at a fort or part of a delay. The reduced event frequency of legs is intentional pacing and balance, not mathematically identical to daily play. Harsh mode does not secretly change that frequency. Keep a leg's event even if the party changes its plan; no event rerolling by switching time scales.

5. Health and continuing to play

People break on this road. The rule that matters is that a broken character never means a benched player.

HP and wounds

Damage reduces HP, minimum 0. When damage first takes a Healthy character to 0, they become Wounded. Wounded characters have Disadvantage on STR/DEX and at 0 cannot perform physical tasks until healed; they can speak and decide. When a later separate damage instance hits them at 0, or returns them to 0 after healing, advance to Gravely Injured. A further separate instance at 0 or return to 0 while Gravely Injured causes death. One damage instance advances at most one step; overflow damage is ignored. Healing HP does not clear a wound state.

Gravely Injured characters cannot take actions/checks; they may speak if the player wishes. They can ride and do not automatically force everyone to stop. An able Doctor or another able traveler can use their daily action (or Camp Action) to stabilize a 0-HP patient without a roll; ordinary stabilization restores 1 HP but does not prevent later damage or change wound state. The Doctor's medicine intervention is stronger and distinct.

At the end of a fed Rest Day, regain 1d4+1 HP, remove 1 Exhaustion, then try CON DC 10 to improve Wound state by one step. On success Gravely → Wounded or Wounded → Healthy. Condition-specific restrictions must be printed. Dead cannot recover. Unfed characters regain 1 HP from rest but remove no Exhaustion and make no Wound recovery check.

Exhaustion

Effects are cumulative. A traveler gains at most 1 Exhaustion per calendar day, from all sources including voluntary risks; no exceptions. Recovery can happen later that day. All removal requires that traveler's full daily ration. Track gains and removals separately.

Level Effect
0 No penalty
1 −1 on checks
2 Disadvantage on physical checks (STR/DEX/CON), replacing the −1 rather than stacking it
3 Disadvantage on all checks; Advantage cannot cancel it
4 Maximum HP halved, round up; clamp current HP to new max without causing a wound
5 Incapacitated; passenger, no actions or checks
6 Death

Party travel is halved once when at least two of the four travelers have Exhaustion 2+. If fewer than two travelers are able to act, wagon travel stops. Wounds alone do not halve speed; unavailable ox teams have their own modifier. Removing Exhaustion 4 restores normal max HP but not lost current HP.

6. Morale, rest, and people

Morale is not a mood. It is the answer to “do we still believe this was worth leaving for?”, asked around a fire, out loud.

Morale is shared, clamped 0–10, starts 7. At 8–10, one group check per session gains Advantage for every participant (players choose before rolling). At 3–4, start the next camp conversation with a disagreement; no automatic check penalty. At 1–2, hold a breaking-point scene. At 0, the party must stop at the next safe camp and renegotiate why it continues; players may spend one Rest Day to reset Morale to 3, or agree to end the expedition. Hostile conditions still need resolving before safe rest. No roll controls a PC or makes them leave.

Lose 1 for a death, breaking an explicit promise to an NPC who learns of it, crossing below 50 lbs food, or crossing below 5 Wagon HP. Each threshold can trigger only once per chapter. Gain 1 for first arrival at a chapter endpoint, fulfilling a significant promise, or a successful Rest Day evening activity. Each named promise rewards once; each evening at most once. No repeated landmark rolls or repeated retelling of a promise for gains.

On any night's camp, invite one short scene: ask for help, admit a fear, challenge a plan, or share something worth carrying. Talking is always available. Rest-only mechanical recovery does not prohibit roleplay on travel nights. No CHA roll is required for an honest conversation.

Rest Days give healing and ox recovery, Teamster upkeep, and one Camp Action per able traveler. Full procedure and actions: Camp Life. Assign three watch shifts; with three able watchers no fatigue. A double shift prompts CON DC 10 or 1 Exhaustion next morning, subject to that day's cap. No watch means a night threat cannot be spotted early; it still gets its printed response, never automatic death.

At forts, the party has safety from random trail events. Weather prevents travel when stated but fort shelter protects people and animals; disease, food, and recovery still apply. Trade uses one day; Rest is separate. Prices and human demands: Trading.

7. Endings and difficulty

Arrival is not a score. It is a date, a list of who is standing, and a set of promises in one of three states: kept, renegotiated, or broken on purpose.

Reach marker 1,900, resolve the final routes, then arrive at 2,000. The finale's stated travel days replace those final 100 miles; never charge both. Failure at the finale offers a defined recovery or walking route, not an unexplained dead end.

Arrival ends the expedition, not all the characters' problems. Resolve Dream, Promise, Bond, and what remains to build with. A character's player authors their intention; circumstances supply one benefit and one complication. Use Arrival. Remember the dead and the people who helped. A rescue, a changed destination, or an agreed retreat can be a complete story, not a score of zero. If the party chooses to winter at a refuge instead of continuing, end this expedition there with the Arrival procedure; a later spring departure is a new campaign, not an unlimited rest-and-wages loop inside this one.

Standard: rules as written. Gentler: $1,000 starting budget; start each chapter with one Inspiration per character; first Wound recovery check each Rest Day has Advantage. Harsh: $650 budget, start May 10, otherwise same rules. Use only after agreement; do not increase DCs and event frequency secretly. Pregens/loadout remain affordable in all modes. 1. The Plains — 0–300

Two hundred wagons on the same grass, all of them leaving at once, all of them certain. The road is easy here and the company is thick. Nobody has learned anything yet.

People who remember

Three wagons that keep turning up on the same road as yours, driven by people with their own reasons for being on it.

Mercy, Tomas, and Nessa are not quest-givers and not rescue services. They are travelers with the same problem the party has — too much to carry, not enough time — who happen to be going the same way. They remember what was done for them and what was not, they tell other people, and the favors they offer are specific, single-use, and limited to what a person in their circumstances could actually provide. Help them because it costs you something. That is the only reason the game rewards.

Version 0.9. These three fictional people travel independently and supply their own parties. They are not extra player actions, stockpiles, or guaranteed rescuers. Introduce their needs before asking for help; tell players when a favor is earned. Record specific promises, favors, and knowledge on the ledger.

Mercy Reed — a schoolteacher with a wagon of books

Wants: to arrive with enough books and printing materials to start a school. Can offer: letters, contacts, organizational work. Will not: abandon a person to save the books. Voice: asks precise questions, then waits for an answer. Her adult brother Jonah drives their wagon; no child peril is needed.

  • Chapter 1: asks the party to carry one crate (one named cargo bundle) until Kearny while a wheel is repaired. Delivering it earns Mercy's introduction: a named contact at Laramie will meet that fort's demand on the party's behalf. Spend once; still roll the bargain.
  • Chapter 2: short $10 for passage. Help with cash or a day's labor. Earn river intelligence: her collected crossing notes lower one finale raft-run DC to 10, once. She can also provide the report needed at Laramie; this is the same information, not extra supplies.
  • Chapter 5: if helped, she leaves a verified water report at the previous stop. It identifies the 90-mile source in chapter 5 as safe, removing the need to investigate that source. It does not refill the party's barrels.
  • Finale: if alive and on good terms, asks which book the party thinks the future school needs. The answer becomes an epilogue connection, not a new automatic reward.

If refused: she sells books to pay passage, arrives with fewer, and remembers honestly without becoming a villain. If a promise is broken: she asks for restitution or an explanation; no omniscient knowledge.

Tomas Fitch — a wheelwright protecting a heavy tool chest

Wants: an independent workshop. Can offer: repairs, work, practical instruction. Will not: lend his last tools without a return agreement. Voice: checks the object while listening to its owner.

  • Chapter 1: willing to exchange one ordinary repair (restore 4 Wagon HP, once) for 20 lbs food; no roll.
  • Chapter 3: needs one day helping brace his own wagon. Doing so earns Tomas's repair favor: at a later meeting he supplies one part for a specific repair or restores 4 Wagon HP. The favor is single-use, not both.
  • Chapter 4: his tool chest makes the shortcut difficult. Carry it as a named bundle to Hall or tell him honestly you cannot. Delivery earns no second mechanical repair favor if one is already held; it fulfills a significant promise for Morale and establishes trust.
  • Finale: he can have left the promised repair part with a named road contact. Establish this at The Dalles before the players choose a route.

If refused: he hires other help and arrives later. If tools are lost: he will accept labor or a replacement plan; the party cannot buy forgiveness with one CHA roll.

Nessa Bell — a carrier trying to keep a delivery promise

Wants: deliver correspondence and paid freight without hiding losses. Can offer: reliable route reports and introductions. Will not: carry sealed dangerous or stolen goods without knowing what she accepts. Voice: writes agreements in a small book and reads them back.

  • Chapter 2: can pass a message to the next fort for $1; gives her name and destination. Messages move with her, not instantaneously.
  • Chapter 4: her Hall delivery gives Ruth at Bridger a reason to offer a bargain. Accepting creates a specific obligation, delivery to Nessa by Hall departure. Arriving late does not make her disappear; a named clerk can receive it.
  • Chapter 5: a freight partner has not arrived. Bring a truthful report from the road or help search one day; this meets Hall's demand.
  • Chapter 6: asks for a day repairing a shared waystation shelter. Helping earns shelter credit: free shared shelter on Barlow day 12. If choosing the river, her carrier contact instead discounts passage by $10. Choose one benefit, once.

If refused: hires help when she can. If lied to: correct the record before seeking another favor. NPC travel follows the fiction; do not place her simultaneously at two forts.

Meeting someone again

Nobody stands still waiting to be met. Between appearances they have travelled, traded, lost things, and heard news — arrive with what changed.

On a recurring-person event choose one whose return is plausible. Ask: What changed for them? What did they hear, and from whom? What do they need that the party cannot solve for free? If no meeting is plausible, find a letter, a marked campsite, or a mutual acquaintance. Favor redemption needs an actual opportunity; a ledger mark never works as magic.

Reserve travelers

Jonah Reed, mechanic Ruth Ames traveling with Tomas, and carrier Ellis Bell traveling with Nessa can serve as temporary/replacement PCs with player permission. Their names, occupations, and connections are fictional. Use the chosen role's fixed-array sheet; no supplies materialize. A fourth reserve can be named by the players. NPC stat blocks are unnecessary until someone becomes an active traveler.

1. Events: warning, choice, consequence

The trail never ambushes anyone. It clears its throat, shows you the bill, and waits.

Version 0.9. The core event selector sends results 1–3 here; roll d6 on the current chapter. Read the warning and both approaches before any roll. Costs replace the day's action when they take a day. Unspecified damage targets the wagon only when labeled Wagon HP; otherwise name the affected traveler before commitment. These events never erase all food, destroy a healthy wagon outright, or kill a healthy team without a response.

Every entry has the same shape, and it is the shape of the whole game: something goes wrong where the party can see it coming, there is a safe way through that costs time or goods, and there is a fast way through that costs the dice. Neither is the correct answer. The correct answer is whichever one the party can live with saying out loud.

A cargo bundle means 50 lbs food, one part, 40 ammunition, one filled extra barrel, or a named personal cargo item. Loss is chosen by players unless the warning identifies the threatened item. If a printed payment is unavailable, use the other option or propose another approach. No repeatable unique reward: an already-resolved landmark becomes ordinary progress.

Making a new event

The craft is in the first sentence. Give the players something they can see, hear, or smell before they have to decide anything — a sound the wagon has started making, a color the sky has turned, a person walking back down the line toward you. If they cannot picture it, they are not making a choice; they are guessing at a rule.

Write a visible warning, one dependable cost, one uncertain approach, full/partial/failure if a group roll, and a way to continue after failure. Name any time, damage, supplies, or obligations precisely. Avoid a hidden later penalty that would have changed the decision. Use an NPC's return or a quiet benefit after two consecutive costly events; replace the next event rather than adding more rolls.

2. Backgrounds and learning

Everyone on this wagon was somebody before Independence. The road does not care, but the party does.

Your role is what the party needs from you. Your background is the life you had before anybody needed anything from you — the winters already survived, the patients already lost, the wagons already mended. A technique is what the trail itself taught you, and it is worth asking who did the teaching: a person, or a failure.

Version 0.9. Choose one background at creation. Advantages do not stack; a use is expended only when its stated benefit applies. Equipment mentioned here is descriptive unless a benefit explicitly supplies it. A chapter is a numbered campaign chapter, not each play session.

Guide

You have been out here before, or you have wanted to be for so long that it amounts to the same thing. The others follow you because someone has to walk in front.

  • Former Mountain Traveler: Advantage on checks to find shelter in chapters 3, 4, and 7.
  • Military Scout: Advantage to spot an approaching physical threat; does not determine another person's intentions or excuse trespass.
  • Driven by a Vision: Once per chapter, after a Morale loss, explain why you still believe in the journey and restore 1 Morale. Another player may disagree without losing the benefit.
  • Cartographer: When using Read the ground, also learn one usable sheltered campsite or safe water source on the chosen route. GM must place one before the next known major obstacle; it cannot erase an entire desert.

After chapters 2 and 5, choose one (no repeats):

  • Surveyor: Read the ground takes half a day rather than one; travel half Standard mileage that day, pay ordinary travel food, and no Rest benefits.
  • Weather Eye: Once per chapter, before committing to a route, see the next weather roll. Record it and use it at the next scheduled weather change; no reroll.
  • Bring Them Through: Once per chapter, on a group navigation check, change one failed participant's result to success after rolls.

Doctor

You know the names of things. That is most of what you have, and on a good day it is enough.

  • Trained in the East: +1 on ordinary disease treatment checks; not recovery checks.
  • Frontier Herbalist: Once per chapter, on a Rest Day in suitable vegetation, gain 1 medicine without spending a Camp Action. These abstract supplies are not a claim about real historical cures.
  • Battle Surgeon: Advantage on ordinary treatment of injuries.
  • Sanitation Keeper: Once per chapter, give all exposed travelers Advantage on one event's initial disease resistance checks before rolling.

Techniques:

  • Steady Hands: Ordinary treatment of one injury succeeds without a roll; still uses the once-daily treatment and has its ordinary effects.
  • Bedside Teacher: On a Rest Day when you use Stabilize, one other traveler can perform ordinary treatment on a different patient, paying normal medicine and making INT DC 10. This is the only extra treatment attempt granted.
  • Watchful Care: Once per chapter, reduce a patient's HP damage from one source by 3, minimum zero, after damage is rolled. You must be present and able to help.

Hunter

The difference between full rations and short ones is you, walking away from the road alone before dawn.

  • Sharpshooter: Ordinary hunting costs 3 ammunition instead of 5; Take Aim still costs 5.
  • Tracker: Advantage on ordinary hunting checks when recent rain or snow has left readable tracks; the GM discloses availability before commitment.
  • Quiet Step: Advantage on your checks to approach animals or avoid observation. This does not make everyone else invisible.
  • Trapper: Once per three-day weather span, spend 1 ammunition and set snares in suitable habitat at camp; gain 5 lbs fresh food next morning without a roll. Mark the use. No yield in terrain without game.

Techniques:

  • Patient Provider: Take Aim yields 40 rather than 30 lbs.
  • Waste Nothing: Your Smoke & Salt action cures up to 100 lbs without rolling, when there is a fire and suitable conditions.
  • Watch the Herd: Once per chapter, prevent 2 HP damage to one ox team from an animal or environmental threat; cannot prevent hard-travel strain.

Teamster

You are the only one who hears the wagon the way it actually sounds. Everyone else finds out when it stops.

  • Blacksmith's Apprentice: Advantage on Field Repair checks. Does not add HP to guaranteed Jury-rig.
  • Ox Whisperer: One living team of your choice restores an extra 1 HP on each Rest Day.
  • Bullwhacker: Advantage on your checks to control a wagon on a slope or in water.
  • Pack-Master: Once per chapter, when an event would take a cargo bundle, protect that bundle through careful packing. Cannot cancel a bundle voluntarily offered as payment or traded for a safer route.

Techniques:

  • Lasting Repair: Jury-rig restores 8 rather than 6 HP.
  • Gentle Driver: Once per three-day weather span, prevent one day's ordinary 1-HP hard-travel strain. In heat, strain drops from 2 to 1 instead.
  • Loadmaster: When the party must walk, each able traveler can carry 35 rather than 25 lbs of supplies. Personal keepsakes/clothes are still free.

Advancing between sessions

Nobody on this road levels up. You get better at the thing you already did, usually because it went badly once.

At chapter end, choose whether a personal statement changed. At chapters 2 and 5 also select a technique, copy its exact wording onto the sheet, and describe who taught you or which failure taught you. Never require a particular NPC to survive to unlock advancement. For a standalone river session, characters begin with no techniques and earn one at its end.

03_settlements_and_trading.md

3. Forts, bargaining, and work

Walls, a posted price list, and someone who wants something from you. A fort is not safety — it is a day when your problems have prices.

The forts are where the journey briefly becomes a world with rules again. Mail moves. Strangers have names and jobs. You can buy your way out of a problem, if you brought enough, and you can find out exactly how much of your margin is left. Everyone at a fort wants something; the useful question is whether what they want is something you were carrying anyway.

Version 0.9. These are fictional game markets. All amounts are design values, not historical quotations.

At arrival, reveal stock, prices, a named trader's demand, and the next known resupply/water point. No roll to learn posted prices or buy offered goods. First arrival at a chapter endpoint grants its one Morale; intermediate Fort Bridger does not give an extra endpoint award. At forts no random trail events occur, shelter is available, and conditions still progress. Trade takes a day at 1 lb food/person; Rest is a separate day at 0.5 lb/person.

Stock and buying

A fort is not a warehouse. It has what it has, the next wagon behind you wants it too, and leaving and coming back does not refill the shelves.

Each fort has 300 lbs cured food, 8 medicine, 4 parts, 100 ammunition, 2 healthy ox teams, 4 empty extra barrels, and 1 Warm Bedding bundle available once per visit. Record purchases; leaving and returning does not reset stock within the chapter. Greater quantities require a stated one-week delivery and prepayment, announced before ordering. Water refill is free, no separate Trade Day if only refilling.

Base prices: food $0.50/lb; medicine $4/dose; parts $6; ammo $0.10/round; ox team $32; extra 10-gallon barrel $3; bedding $30. Multiply by the fort rate, then the discount, round the final bill to cents. Included starting tools can be replaced as a set for $10 base if lost in a named event.

One bargain roll per visit: choose CHA or INT against the listed DC. Meeting the demand gives −4 DC. Success: 15% off purchases today. Beat DC by 5: 25% off instead. Failure: posted prices, no insult or morale damage. Do not roll to find out if an accepted price is honored. An unfulfilled accepted delivery creates an obligation, not a secret price change.

Barter surplus at half local posted value, no discount on what the trader buys. Use the credit against the discounted purchase bill. Fractions of supplies are allowed for food; parts, medicine, teams, and barrels are whole units. Fresh food accepted only if still edible, at half cured food's base value before the usual resale calculation. NPCs are not obligated to buy limitless game: maximum food buyback 50 lbs per visit. No self-financing buy/sell loop.

Work and rescue

Money runs out before the road does. When it happens, the answer is a week of somebody else's labor — and everyone at the table should hear which week, and whose.

One able traveler can earn $3 × fort multiplier per labor day, guaranteed. At most two travelers may work on a given day. Those working consume ordinary non-travel food and get no Rest recovery/actions; others may individually rest. Party travel is zero. Deduct each person's own day's ration; do not grant whole-party healing because one person rests.

A stranded party can accept one rescue contract per campaign: a local carrier provides passage to the next fort for the travelers and up to 100 lbs supplies, in exchange for a clearly named future seven-day labor obligation. No wagon or teams are conjured. Advance seven days, paying no food from the party pool (included passage); apply condition recovery but no random events or Rest healing. An incapacitated traveler receives ordinary care (1 HP/day, no Wound recovery); substitute players keep acting in conversation. This escape route preserves the story while sacrificing equipment and time.

Worked purchase

At Laramie, Owen accepts the crossing report, reducing DC 12 to 8. Mae rolls INT total 10: 15% discount. Buying 40 lbs food ($20 base), 2 medicine ($8), and 1 part ($6) costs $34 × 2 × 0.85 = $57.80. Selling 20 rounds first provides $2 × 2 × 0.5 = $2 credit, so cash due is $55.80. Deduct the sold ammo, mark the fort's reduced stock, and pay the Trade Day's four-person food ration separately.

04_native_encounters.md

4. Indigenous communities and encounters

The land the party is crossing is somebody's Tuesday.

Every mile of this route runs through places where people already live and work — where the fishing site is maintained, the crossing is operated, the stock is counted, and the road is one more thing that happened this year. The travelers are the newcomers here, and usually the ones who need something. Play these scenes the way you would play any scene with a competent adult who has their own day to get through: they have work in progress, terms they will state plainly, and no obligation to explain themselves to four strangers with a wagon.

The design consequence is simple. There is no hostility score, no reaction roll, and no mechanism by which a bad die makes someone dangerous. What is uncertain in these scenes is never what kind of person is this — it is what are the travelers willing to offer, and can they live with the answer.

Version 0.9. Use alongside historical framing and the agreed session boundaries. The route passes through inhabited lands. Indigenous people have their own livelihoods, priorities, relationships, and authority; their presence is not an obstacle category.

Do not infer a nation's behavior from a die roll, invent a purported traditional ceremony, or make one person's goodwill apply to unrelated communities. A generic successful exchange cannot buy a universal “tribal reputation” bonus. The scenarios below are fictional encounter frameworks, not descriptions of a specific nation's customs or documented 1848 events. Before assigning a real community, consult sources from that community and establish the actual location. Until then use the individual's occupation and a mutually understood name rather than inventing Indigenous-language names or claiming cultural authenticity.

Prepare an individual, not a hostility score

Half an index card is enough, and it is the same card you would write for a ferryman or a fort clerk.

Write their name or how they ask to be addressed, their current work, what they want today, what they will not agree to, what they can actually offer, and whether/how the party could meet again. They can disagree with other members of their community. They need not justify boundaries to the travelers or be persuaded to abandon them.

Ordinary greetings, paying a stated fee, accepting a warning, and agreeing a fair trade succeed without rolls. If translation is difficult, allow gestures, an available interpreter, time, or a simpler agreement. Never roll for whether somebody accidentally commits an unspecified cultural offense.

Six encounter frames

Each is a person mid-task who has something the party may want, and a limit they have already decided on. Read the offer and the boundary as stated facts, not as an opening bid to be worn down.

Choose one appropriate to place and current circumstances. These can replace a random event; no requirement to have exactly one “Native encounter” per chapter.

d6 Person and purpose Offer / boundary Meaningful choice
1 A river worker finishing paid passage for another group Next day's guided crossing for $10 or one part; current customers go first. Wait and pay for safe passage, or choose a separately described crossing with known risks. Paying requires no social roll.
2 A household protecting a damaged fishing site Travelers must move camp; repair damage only with their permission. Move, spending half a travel day, or devote a day to agreed repair and move afterward. Neither buys continued access.
3 A trader with cured food and limited time Offers 40 lbs cured food for 2 medicine or 80 rounds. Will not sell more today. Trade scarce supplies, offer a clearly useful alternative, or decline peacefully. No special food multipliers.
4 A messenger concerned about a washed-out approach Gives the warning freely; requests delivery of a response to a named person at the next trading stop. Accept a specific obligation or thank them and decline. The warning remains true either way.
5 A stock keeper looking for a missing animal Wants reliable information, not accusations. Spend half a travel day investigating tracks (WIS DC 10); success provides a location, failure loses the time only. No duty to pursue into danger.
6 A craft worker who needs dry storage while traveling Offers one future wagon repair (restore 4 HP, once) in return for protecting a cargo bundle to the next fort. Accept and record a delivery, or keep the space and responsibility free. Their own travel and food are independent.

A proposal outside the printed terms can need one CHA or INT DC 10 check only if the other person is genuinely open to it. Success secures the alternative; failure leaves original terms available. No check makes forbidden access acceptable. Failing to reach agreement means no deal, departure, a detour, or another disclosed cost; it does not create a firefight.

Record promises and consent on the people ledger. When an agreement is fulfilled, the specific person may offer a specific future favor if they can. Name it and its limits. Broken trust follows information: people cannot know what they have not learned.

Coercion or violence

Violence is never a random social-check outcome. If someone deliberately initiates force, pause, confirm the table's boundaries, and state the danger. Offer withdrawal with restitution or another credible exit. Use the same rules for any armed confrontation, regardless of who is involved.

If the group proceeds: group DEX or WIS DC 14 to escape, not to conquer. Full success: escape, leave one cargo bundle. Partial: escape, leave one bundle and each participant takes 1d4 HP damage. Failure: escape after two lost travel days, leave two bundles, each participant takes 1d4 damage. Apply normal wounds; no automatic execution, conquest reward, or universal hostility increase. If escape is impossible in the established fiction, agree a different scene and stakes before rolling; do not improvise captivity that removes a player from the game.

Review boundary

These revisions remove the automatic failed-diplomacy-to-violence structure. They do not establish historical accuracy or Indigenous review. 05_camp_life.md

5. Camp life and recovery

The day's accounting is finished. The fire is lit. This is the only part of the journey these people get to keep.

Camp is where the game gives back. Wounds close, oxen recover, the wagon gets another day of life out of a Teamster with a part and an hour. But the reason to slow down here is not the healing — it is that a person who has spent all day being a Hunter finally gets to be whoever they are when nobody needs feeding. Talking is always available, on any night, at no cost. Not every evening needs to become a confession.

Version 0.9. At every night's camp, invite one conversation. At a Rest Day, also apply recovery and Camp Actions. Conversation never needs a day's mechanical rest to become available.

Rest Day procedure

Name a safe or at least usable stopping place; disclose weather hazards. Pay 0.5 lb food per traveler (2 lbs for the four-person party) and ordinary water if tracked. No miles. Each able traveler takes one Camp Action. Only one repair action can target the wagon; Teamster upkeep is additional. At day's end resolve condition checks, heal each fed traveler 1d4+1 HP, remove 1 Exhaustion, and roll CON DC 10 to improve each fed wounded traveler's wound state by one step. Unfed travelers heal 1 HP only. Each living team heals 1 HP; Teamster restores 1 Wagon HP automatically. Effects cannot exceed maximums.

Field Repair STR DC 10; restore 1d4+2 Wagon HP capped at 15. On success may spend 1 part to instead restore 1d6+3 up to max. Failure consumes no part.
Jury-rig (Teamster) Spend 1 part, restore 6 Wagon HP guaranteed, up to max. Replaces Field Repair.
Preventative Maintenance STR DC 10; success stores protection against the next wagon-damage instance: reduce it by 3 HP, minimum zero. Mark until used; no stacking.

The Doctor can still perform their once-daily ordinary treatment alongside a Camp Action unless they use Stabilize; that uses both the Camp Action and their treatment for the day. An ordinary traveler stabilizing someone at 0 HP uses their Camp Action, restores 1 HP, and does not perform disease treatment.

One evening activity per Rest Day

Choose one. These cannot stack on the same evening.

  • Tell the truth: share or summarize something another traveler did not know; if at least two characters respond, Morale +1. No acting test, no CHA check, maximum once per chapter for this activity.
  • Proper meal: spend an extra full Rest Day ration for everyone (2 additional lbs for four). Morale +1; no additional Exhaustion removal.
  • Write or remember: name a person you hope hears the story; one character who lacks Inspiration gains it. This does not consume that character's once-per-session personal-stakes award.

Watch

Somebody stays awake. The watch does not prevent the night from happening — it buys the party the chance to answer it.

Assign three shifts to able travelers. One shift has no penalty. Taking two shifts requires CON DC 10 next morning or 1 Exhaustion, counted against the new day's cap. An incapacitated traveler cannot watch. If nobody watches, the party misses a night event's early warning; present its immediate response choices anyway. Watch protects the chance to respond, not a hidden automatic success.

Camp prompts

Use one when conversation needs a start. Pass freely. These are questions about people, not tests of performance — a shrug and one sentence is a complete answer.

  1. Who knows you are concealing pain?
  2. What did someone do today that changed your opinion of them?
  3. What would you ask the people back home to forgive?
  4. Who deserves a portion of what you hope to build?
  5. Which object would you save after the food?
  6. What does another traveler misunderstand about your promise?
  7. Who do you miss who would have disliked this journey?
  8. What did you enjoy today despite everything?

A pause, a joke, and an ordinary kindness belong here too. Every evening need not become a confession.

06_weather_and_environment.md

6. Weather and environment

You can argue with a ferryman. You can outwork a broken axle. The sky is the one thing at this table that will not be negotiated with.

Weather is the game's honest antagonist: impersonal, announced in advance, and identical for the prepared and the unprepared except in what it costs them. It turns every three days on the calendar, not because the story wants a blizzard. Read it out loud at dawn, before anyone chooses a stance — the point is never the surprise, it is watching a party decide what a hard sky is worth going out into.

Version 0.9. Roll d6 every three calendar days and on entry to a new chapter (restart the three-day count). Use the current month. Apply the condition on each day of its span unless the entry states otherwise. A weather table entry is not an additional random event. Roll recovery/exposure in batches during travel legs, but stop when someone becomes incapacitated or players need a decision.

Shelter

Canvas keeps rain off supplies. It does not keep November off people. Knowing the difference is most of what survives a cold chapter.

Forts and established substantial shelter protect people, wagon, and teams from weather damage. A normal canvas camp protects belongings from ordinary rain but is not automatically shelter against severe wind/cold. Before hazardous weather, the Guide may seek cover, WIS DC 10 (DC 12 in open desert/plains); one attempt per day, no separate action. Success shelters the whole party and stock for that day/night. Failure reveals the exposure before travel: the party may use its day's action to build secure shelter instead, guaranteed, with zero miles and no Rest benefits that first day. Subsequent days there can be Rest Days. Abandoning that site loses the benefit. If a cold threat is first revealed at night, allow the search immediately. On failure, reveal the exposure and let the party establish emergency cover at the cost of half the following day’s mileage; no Rest benefits for that half day. A threat cannot deny shelter choices merely because its warning arrived late. Tools/canvas are included in the wagon; without them find an established site instead.

Warm Bedding protects travelers from cold exposure checks/damage, not animals or wagon. Sheltered people are also protected from cold. A scene may explicitly threaten bedding as a cargo choice; wagon reaching 0 HP does not itself erase the bedding.

7. Routes and the final choice

Two ways down out of the mountains. One is fast and takes the wagon away from you. One keeps the wagon and takes the time. Neither is the safe one.

This is where the campaign collects its debts. Every promise still open, every favor earned and unspent, every bundle somebody insisted on keeping since Independence — it all comes to the same table at The Dalles, and the party divides what arrives from what does not. Show both routes in full before anyone pays for either. The last stretch of this journey should be decided by people who understood the offer, not by people who guessed.

Version 0.9. Use the fictionalized map. Costs are game values. The GM states them before commitment. Route promises may be incomplete, but the Guide can investigate, and a false claim must have discoverable evidence.

Sublette decision, marker 790

Seventy miles against a fort you promised somebody you would reach. The road forks; the obligation does not.

Present before Fort Bridger (850): established route with trade and a promised delivery, or a shortcut skipping that fort. This is the chapter 4 major decision, replacing that day's random event.

  • Established: continue through Bridger and marker 900 normally. Keep access to the fort and its services.
  • Shortcut: saves 70 miles; after completing a 40-mile waterless stretch, advance the route marker by an additional 70. Do not add calendar days for the skipped miles.   • Forecast: pay ordinary water each travel/wait day, 4 gallons in heat. The shortcut has no refill until rejoining. GM shows the current mileage/water estimate including speed penalties before departure. No additional automatic CON check or stock damage; ordinary weather and one event for the crossing apply.

If a promise needs Bridger, the party can delay the shortcut, ask another named traveler to deliver, or openly break it. Delegation requires their agreement; an NPC is not a guaranteed remote action. Record the consequence.

The false cutoff rumor

Somebody on this road is always sure they know a better way. This one is wrong, and the game does not punish the party for listening — only for refusing to check.

A traveler claims a second shortcut saves weeks. This is fictional rumor content, not a runnable Hastings route in this Oregon campaign. Finale at The Dalles, marker 1,900

One hundred miles left, and the wagon that carried everything this far is now itself a thing to be spent or kept.

Reveal both routes before anyone pays. These fixed-duration journeys replace the last 100 miles. Use weather/food/conditions on their days but no random trail events: the three prepared obstacles below are the finale's events. Two weeks over the mountain, a toll at the gate, and a descent at Laurel Hill that has taken better wagons than yours. You keep everything you brought — if it keeps up.

Arrival

Arrival: what the journey made possible

The wagon stops. Somebody has to be the first to climb down.

Arrival is not a win condition and there is no last roll. It is a date, a list of who is standing, a ledger of promises in one of three states, and a room full of people finding out what all of it was for. Every ending here is authored by a player — the record supplies the circumstances, and they supply the intention.

Version 0.9. Allow about 20 minutes, longer if the table wants it. Use after reaching the Valley, being rescued, choosing retreat, or agreeing another destination. No final roll invalidates arrival.

1. Put the record on the table

Say the numbers out loud before anyone says what they mean. The ledger is the evidence; the meaning belongs to the table.

Read the date, destination, surviving travelers, graves, named allies, unfulfilled promises, remaining money/supplies, and whether wagon, teams, and personal cargo arrived. These are facts, not a numerical ranking. Say whose help mattered. Count replacements as people with their own futures, not disguised original survivors.

2. Give each player the future

Ask in turn:

  • What did you come to build, and do you still want it?
  • Which promise do you fulfill, renegotiate, or knowingly leave unfinished?
  • Who shares in what happens next?
  • What part of the journey do you tell differently than someone else?

The player states their intention. Together choose one earned benefit and one unresolved complication from the record. No die roll decides a PC's beliefs, marriage, profession, lifespan, or whether their dream was foolish.

Journey evidence Possible benefit Possible complication
Tools/cargo preserved Begin the intended work with equipment. It needs customers, premises, or materials.
Strong specific relationship A named person offers an introduction or shared work. They have a need of their own.
Early arrival / supplies intact Time or reserves to prepare. Another traveler asks for help from that margin.
Late arrival / little money Paid work and temporary lodging are offered. The original project must wait.
Promise broken A chance to tell the truth and make restitution. Trust returns through action, not one roll.
Wounded or chronically affected Adapt the work and accept support the player chooses. Recovery takes time; do not assign a humiliating or predetermined life.

Dates inform circumstances, not automatic death. Buying a house, finding employment, or partnering on a project can be an ending. Do not automatically grant land or treat Indigenous dispossession as a victory prize. Specific historical land-law claims require research outside this fictional resolution.

3. Remember the dead

The graves are the part of the journey nobody chose, and the part everyone keeps.

For each grave, name the mile, date, and one thing the survivors do differently because of that person. The former player chooses whether to add a memory. For a departed living PC, say what is known and leave unknowns open. There is no forced tragic epilogue roll.

4. Close on an action

Not a summary of a life. One thing somebody does the following morning.

Each survivor describes one concrete first act: hang a sign, deliver a letter, seek a person, repair a roof, plant something, ask for work. The final image belongs to the players.

If an optional prompt helps, roll d6: 1 a familiar handwriting; 2 a repaired object; 3 someone saying your name correctly; 4 a door opened; 5 an honest debt; 6 a meal shared. Prompts suggest a scene, not its outcome.

Then ask out of character: What choice will you remember? Record it for the playtest. Do not ask players to endorse the design target rating.

Worked procedures — v0.9

Six moments played out in full, so the arithmetic is never the thing slowing a table down. Read them when a rule feels ambiguous, not before your first session.

These are constructed examples, not observed playtest results. Use the core when resolving a new interaction.

1. A river decision that costs something

Use The River Between's standalone snapshot: June 2, marker 340, food 140 cured, wagon 22, three teams 8 HP each, money$25, medicine 4, parts 2. Weather Clear and Mild. All four travelers are healthy, full 9 HP, Exhaustion 0.

The group chooses the upper ford immediately. Before dice the GM reads DC 12 and all three outcomes. Players make DEX/WIS checks: Eliza total 15, Thomas 8, Cal 13, Mae 9. Two successes among four is partial, not full success. They cross; wagon loses 2 HP. They choose losing Mae's tool chest instead of a volunteer taking 1d4 damage. Mae decides her dream survives but she must borrow tools to begin it. Ordinary wagon tools remain; personal cargo and included tools are distinct.

The crossing uses one ordinary Travel Day: 4 lbs food, no extra generic river strain, no second event. End state: marker 358, food 136, wagon 20, teams 8/8/8, $25, medicine 4, parts 2. June 3 is next morning. Cal's Boundary influenced the decision; if it created a meaningful personal complication, award his available Inspiration award, but he cannot hold more than one. Mae may also qualify through her Promise's cost; no automatic morale penalty for an internal promise's change. If it was a specific NPC promise and that person learns it was broken, apply the core rule then.

Everyone participates in the far-bank conversation. The group can continue or Rest tomorrow; a failure would instead have left them on the near bank with the ferry still available.

2. A Rest Day with treatment and preservation

Separate state: July 20, marker 950, hot/dry desert, water 20/60, food 136 cured +40 fresh (caught July 18, expires after July 21 evening), wagon 20/30, teams 8/8/8, medicine 4, parts 2. Cal has HP 4/9, Exhaustion 1, dysentery with 3 marks and damage due today. Others are full HP, no wounds. A sheltered spring is not present; they are resting under canvas with adequate carried water. No random event is due because this is the second day of an already generated delay that permits Rest.

  • Thomas uses Stabilize on Cal: medicine 4→3; consumes Thomas's Camp Action and daily treatment. Dysentery's 1 HP damage today is prevented. At day's end remove 2 marks, leaving 1; no dysentery recovery roll tonight.
  • Mae uses Jury-rig: parts 2→1, wagon 20→26. Her free upkeep adds 1, ending 27. No second repair check is allowed today.
  • Cal uses Smoke & Salt, WIS roll 11: all 40 fresh food becomes cured. Eliza Scouts, WIS total 12, earning the stated next-navigation benefit.
  • Pay 2 lbs food, leaving 174 cured, 0 fresh; hot weather uses 4 gallons, leaving 16. Nobody gains Exhaustion from mere low stores.
  • Cal's healing die is 3: heals 4 HP, ending 8/9; fed rest removes Exhaustion 1→0. Other healing clamps at max. No one has a Wound state to check.
  • All teams heal 1 to 9; Mae's Ox Whisperer adds 1 to team A, so 10/9/9.
  • Evening: Write or remember grants Inspiration to a character currently lacking it. No Morale increase from that activity, and no extra food cost.

Final changes: food 174 cured, water 16, wagon 27, medicine 3, parts 1, teams 10/9/9, Cal 8 HP/Exhaustion 0/dysentery 1 mark. July 21 begins. The stabilization benefit expired; Cal needs the remaining mark cleared and may take disease damage tomorrow unless treated again.

3. A three-day leg, without three narrated scenes

Separate healthy state: July 10, marker 910, hot/dry weather newly rolled (three days remaining), two usable teams, food 100 cured, water 60, no wounds or conditions. Standard stance. Event selector 6 gives quiet progress.

Three days: 18 +18 +18 = 54 miles, food 4 ×3 = 12 lbs, water 4 ×3 = 12 gallons. End marker 964, food 88, water 48; next morning July 13, new weather due. No hard-travel strain, no hidden Travel check. A conversation can happen once during the montage; no extra event for talking.

If a prepared obstacle were at 950, day 3 would end there after 14 miles, not 964; remaining miles are not banked. If day 1's event revealed a major choice, stop after resolving day 1 and leave the other two days unspent. A leg does not prepay healing, time, or progress.

4. Damage at zero and immediate participation

A traveler with 2 HP takes 4 damage: HP becomes 0 and Healthy→Wounded, no extra step for overflow. Before healing, a separate 1-HP injury advances Wounded→Gravely. Another distinct damage instance while still 0 would be fatal. An able companion instead uses their action to stabilize: HP 1, still Gravely, still unable to act.

The player immediately takes the reserve traveler for the next scene. The patient may be cared for in an allied wagon (agreed, so four people remain in the active wagon) or ride with the group (explicitly count five bodies for food). Only the four active slots provide actions and role benefits. The patient can still have dialogue and a future. No one pays for permission to play.

5. Late travel and water warning

One usable team, two travelers at Exhaustion 2, Standard stance, clear weather: 18 ×0.75 ×0.5 =6.75, rounded once → 7 miles/day. With 40 gallons in heat, water covers 10 days but only 70 miles at this pace. A 200-mile stretch is not safe simply because someone bought two barrels. Show the estimate before departure, then discuss rest, more water, another team, or the guaranteed-source detour.

6. A peaceful disagreement

A trader offers 40 lbs cured food for 2 medicine. The party proposes 40 rounds instead of 80. The trader is open to a smaller sale but will not lower the full quantity's price. State terms: 20 lbs for 40 rounds, no roll. If they instead make a plausible new offer the trader is open to, CHA DC 10 can settle that offer. Failure leaves the original terms; the party can decline. No insult roll, standoff, or automatic violence follows.

docs/SESSION_ZERO.md

Before the wagon leaves

Five minutes in a room, before anybody is anywhere near a river.

This game puts real losses in front of people — hunger, illness, graves, promises broken on purpose. That works only when everyone agreed in advance to the kind of story it is, and when anyone can stop it without explaining themselves. Settle that first. It takes less time than assigning ability scores.

Version 0.9. Read this aloud or summarize in five minutes; invite changes before play.

“We are playing ordinary adults sharing a wagon in a fictionalized 1848 journey. Supplies and time matter. Characters can die, but players always keep a meaningful role. We will hear catastrophic stakes before choosing them. The story includes other people's homes and livelihoods; the land is not empty.”

Agree one tone: hopeful hardship (default), sober survival, or harsh survival. Use the corresponding core difficulty only by agreement; tone is not permission for surprise punishment. Default four travelers even with fewer players; share the extra roles.

Default boundaries: no depicted sexual violence; no child harm; no required slurs or reenactment of racist abuse; illness/injury described briefly rather than graphically; no PC-versus-PC violence or mind control. People can discuss the historical setting's injustice without performing abuse at the table. Change these boundaries only through explicit agreement; anyone can ask for stricter limits privately.

At any time a player may say pause, skip, or rewind. Stop, remove or revise the content, and continue without requiring an explanation or charging a mechanical cost. A player can pass on a personal question or describe an action without acting it out. Players decide their characters' convictions and departures.

Choose each character's Dream, Promise, Boundary, and Bond with the other players' consent. Explain illness, death, temporary substitutes, and reserve travelers now. Nobody sits out because the party cannot afford a replacement fee.

Name who tracks the wagon and who tracks the calendar; rotate if desired. Make text readable, allow digital dice and spoken choices, and share arithmetic. Arrange breaks and a stopping time. No player needs to justify a break through their character's condition.

Finish with: What would make this journey worth playing for you? Name one topic to include and, privately if preferred, one to exclude. These are ongoing agreements, not a waiver signed at the start.

docs/SOLO.md

Solo: four voices, one expedition

Four people who disagree, and one person deciding what the wagon does.

Playing alone works because the interesting part was never the GM's secrets — it was the argument at the fork. Give each traveler their sentence, let the relevant expert state the facts, and then choose as the party. The oracle exists only for questions the prepared material genuinely does not answer.

Version 0.9. Use the four pregens and core procedures. Prepared scene facts, prices, and consequences stay fixed; the oracle never overwrites them. Read only the current chapter ahead if you prefer discovery, but reveal risk before commitment just as a GM would.

Council

At a major decision write one sentence per traveler based on Dream/Promise/Boundary. Let the relevant expert explain the facts. Choose the party plan yourself; majority voting is optional and cannot force a character's beliefs. Resolve all four eligible rolls normally. A scene at camp can be four short diary lines.

Unknown facts

Ask a specific yes/no question only when no rule or prepared fact answers it. Roll d6: likely yes on 2–6, even chance yes on 4–6, unlikely yes on 6. Decide likelihood from established fiction before rolling. On a 1 or 6, add a small complication or benefit only if plausible; no free supplies or catastrophic loss without the normal warned choice.

For an uncertain NPC response, consult their stated boundary first. If open to an alternative, use the normal negotiation check. For Cautious events choose the milder one yourself; for Reckless choose the one that most threatens your current plan, stating why before resolving it. If equal, use the first.

End a session

Record date, marker, weather span days remaining, next decision, all resources and conditions, and one sentence each traveler has not yet said aloud. Continue from that record. An incapacitated role gets its reserve immediately under core rules. Do not invent extra actions for NPCs merely because there is no human GM.

Start with The River Between. Use its actual alternatives, play the failed-ford recovery if it occurs, and record how often you needed to invent a missing rule. Solo completion is useful procedure feedback, not proof of multiplayer engagement.

docs/HISTORY.md

Historical framing and sources

Version 0.9. Source check: September 25, 2026. This is a fictional survival game situated in 1848. It is not a reenactment, curriculum, medical guide, or claim of Indigenous consultation.

What is researched, and what is invented

The broad westward emigrant journey, use of river corridors, existing Indigenous presence, and river/Barlow alternatives are grounded in the sources below. The seven equalized narrative chapters, approximate 2,000-mile progress scale, specific fort markers, playable water sources, prices, carrying limits, timelines, medical mechanics, characters, and adventure incidents are design abstractions or fiction. Do not repeat them as historical facts.

National Park Service material describes the journey through river corridors and cautions against the popular picture of wagons circling primarily against Indigenous attack. That informs the game's environmental and cooperative encounter emphasis, rather than serving as permission to generalize every interaction. Traveling the Emigrant Trails.

The NPS overview explicitly places the emigrant journey in lands long traversed by American Indians. The setting therefore treats communities as existing people with their own futures, not anonymous frontier scenery. History & Culture.

The historical Barlow Road supplied an overland alternative to the Columbia route. The game's tolls and seven/fourteen-day finale durations are invented for choices at the table; they are not quotations of documented rates or travel times. The Barlow Road.

For regional perspectives, begin with the Confederated Tribes of the Umatilla Indian Reservation's own account of Cayuse, Umatilla, and Walla Walla history, rather than assigning a generic “tribal” attitude. CTUIR: Brief History. For the Fort Hall region, use the Shoshone-Bannock Tribes' own government resources and distinguish the later reservation/treaty history from the game's 1848 setting. Shoshone-Bannock Tribes.

Questions still requiring specialist review

  • Exact route geography, seasonal travel, water sources, fort operation, and market practices in 1848.
  • Nation-specific locations, languages, relationships, economies, and the effects of emigrant movement.
  • The consequences of colonization and dispossession in how arrival is framed.
  • Any future real-person portrayal, historical name, illustration, ceremonial detail, or legal land claim.

A small source check does not validate every fact. The generic encounter frameworks intentionally avoid fabricated cultural practices; they still need informed review before becoming a public historical product. README.md

Oregon Trail RPG

What are we willing to spend, and what do we refuse to lose, to get these people to Oregon?

Four ordinary people put everything they own into one wagon and point it west. Nobody in that wagon is a hero. One of them is going because of a promise made to a dying woman. One is going because there is nothing left behind them worth the return trip. They will not all agree about what to do at the river, and they will not all arrive.

This is a survival tabletop RPG about the 2,000 miles between wanting a life and having one — a fictionalized 1848 journey where the calendar is a countdown, the food barrel is an argument waiting to happen, and every mile is paid for in something. You choose what to risk, whom to help, and which promises survive contact with the road. Death is permanent. Every player keeps playing.

Version 0.9 — playtest edition. One GM and 1–4 players controlling four travelers, or solo. Seven chapters, targeting 6–8 sessions; duration and balance await real-table evidence. Familiar d20 checks, no D&D books required.
